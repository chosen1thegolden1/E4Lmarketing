# E4L Vault — Prompt Style Guide

Every prompt in the Vault follows this guide, so the library feels like one product.

## Who the prompts are for
- Small business owners doing their own marketing (local services, coaches, e-commerce, med spas, contractors, real estate).
- People earning with AI who do marketing work for clients (E4L Activate students).
- Most use the free version of ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. No prompt may require a paid plan, a plugin or web browsing.

## Format (Markdown, exactly this shape for every prompt)

```
### [ID] [Title]

**Use it when:** one sentence, the situation the member is in.
**You'll get:** one sentence, the concrete output.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
[the prompt]
```

**Pro tip:** one sentence that makes the result better (a follow-up line to send, what to paste in, how to test it).
```

- IDs: category letter + two digits, e.g. `AD-01`, `EM-07`, `CT-12`, `OF-03`, `AN-20`.
- Titles say the result, not the technique: "Write 3 Facebook ads from one offer", not "Ad copy framework".

## How the prompts themselves are written
1. **Role first, with a believable, specific expertise**, not "world-class" or "the best in the world". Example: "You are a direct-response copywriter who has written Facebook ads for local service businesses for 10 years."
2. **Fill-in blanks in square brackets and capitals**: `[BUSINESS]`, `[OFFER]`, `[AUDIENCE]`, `[PRICE]`, `[CITY]`. Put an example after a slash when it helps: `[OFFER / e.g. $49 drain cleaning this month]`.
3. **Ask the AI to ask first when context is missing.** Many prompts end with: "Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered. Wait for my answers."  Use this on prompts where the output depends heavily on the business (strategy, offers, sequences). Skip it for quick tasks.
4. **Numbered deliverables.** Say exactly what comes back: how many variations, word counts, structure.
5. **Rules that kill generic output.** Every prompt includes a short "Rules:" block. Pick from:
   - Talk about the customer's problem before the offer.
   - Use specific numbers, places and details from what I gave you. Don't invent statistics, reviews or results.
   - Write like a person talking to one person. Short sentences. No jargon.
   - Never use these words: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust, "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "take it to the next level".
   - No em dashes. No hashtags unless asked. No emojis unless asked.
6. **End with a next step** when useful: "Then tell me which one you'd test first and why."
7. **Honest claims only.** Prompts must never tell the AI to fabricate testimonials, fake scarcity, fake reviews or made-up results. If proof is needed, use a blank: `[A REAL RESULT OR TESTIMONIAL, if you have one]`.
8. Length: most prompts 80–220 words. Long enough to be specific, short enough to scan.

## Tone of the surrounding text (Use it when / You'll get / Pro tip)
Plain, friendly, direct. Second person. No hype. Sentences under 20 words.

## Platform notes
- Ads: Meta (Facebook/Instagram) first, Google second. Respect ad policy: no "you" statements about personal attributes (health, finances, etc.), no before/after promises.
- Email: assume a small list (under 5,000). Plain-text style emails.
- Content: short vertical video, Instagram/Facebook posts, LinkedIn for B2B.
- GoHighLevel can be mentioned as one option, never required.
