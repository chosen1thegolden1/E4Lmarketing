# Email & Text

These prompts write the emails and texts that bring in bookings, sales and repeat customers, from welcome sequences to the quick text that saves a missed call. Fill in the blanks, paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and edit the result so it sounds like you before you send.

### EM-01 Write a 5-email welcome sequence for new subscribers

**Use it when:** people are joining your list and you have nothing set up to greet them.
**You'll get:** five ready-to-load welcome emails with subject lines, send timing and one clear action each.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are an email marketer who has built welcome sequences for small local businesses and solo service providers for 8 years.

Write a 5-email welcome sequence for new subscribers to [BUSINESS / e.g. Bright Smile Family Dental in Tulsa].

Details:
- What they signed up for: [LEAD MAGNET OR REASON / e.g. 10% off first cleaning]
- Who they are: [AUDIENCE]
- Main offer I want them to take: [OFFER AND PRICE]
- What makes us different: [2 OR 3 REAL DIFFERENCES]
- Proof I can use: [A REAL RESULT OR TESTIMONIAL, if you have one]

Deliver:
1. A one-line plan showing the goal and send day for each email (day 0, 1, 3, 5, 7).
2. Each email with 2 subject line options, preview text, and a body of 100 to 180 words.
3. Email 1 delivers what they signed up for. Email 5 makes a direct offer.

Rules:
- Talk about the reader's problem before the offer.
- Plain-text style. Short paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences. One link or call to action per email.
- Use the details I gave you. Don't invent statistics, reviews or results.
- Write like a person talking to one person. No jargon.
- Never use: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust.
- No em dashes. No emojis.

Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered. Wait for my answers.
```

**Pro tip:** Send yourself the whole sequence and read it on your phone before you load it.

### EM-02 Write a sales email for one offer with 5 subject lines

**Use it when:** you have one offer to push this week and need an email that sells it.
**You'll get:** one plain-text sales email, a short P.S., and 5 subject lines in different styles.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are a direct-response copywriter who writes sales emails for small businesses with lists under 5,000 people.

Write one sales email for this offer:
- Business: [BUSINESS]
- Offer: [OFFER / e.g. $49 drain cleaning this month]
- Who it's for: [AUDIENCE]
- The problem it solves: [PROBLEM IN THE CUSTOMER'S WORDS]
- Real deadline or limit, if any: [DEADLINE OR "none"]
- Proof: [A REAL RESULT OR TESTIMONIAL, if you have one]
- Where to buy or book: [LINK OR PHONE]

Deliver:
1. 5 subject lines: one curiosity, one direct benefit, one question, one with a number, one that sounds like a note from a friend. Each under 45 characters.
2. Preview text, under 90 characters.
3. The email: 150 to 250 words. Open with the problem, then the offer, then exactly what to do next.
4. A P.S. that restates the offer in one sentence.

Rules:
- Use specific numbers and details from what I gave you. Don't invent results or scarcity.
- If I said "none" for the deadline, do not create urgency.
- Short sentences. One call to action.
- No em dashes. No emojis. No jargon.

Then tell me which subject line you'd test first and why.
```

**Pro tip:** Read the email out loud; any sentence you stumble on, cut or shorten.

### EM-03 Write 20 subject lines and pick which to test first

**Use it when:** your email is written but the subject line feels flat.
**You'll get:** 20 subject lines sorted by style, plus a ranked shortlist of 3 to test.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are an email marketer who has run subject line tests for small business newsletters and promotions for 8 years.

Here is my email:
[PASTE YOUR EMAIL]

My audience: [AUDIENCE / e.g. homeowners in Boise who used us once]
What I want them to do: [GOAL / e.g. book a fall furnace check]

Deliver:
1. 20 subject lines in 5 groups of 4: Curiosity, Direct benefit, Question, Number or specific detail, Personal note.
2. Keep every line under 50 characters. Mark any line under 30 characters with (short).
3. A preview text line for each group.
4. Your top 3 to test, ranked, with one sentence each on why.

Rules:
- Every subject line must match what is actually in the email. No bait-and-switch.
- Use specific details from my email, like prices, places or dates.
- No ALL CAPS words, no "FREE!!!", no fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- No em dashes. No emojis.
- Never use: unlock, elevate, game-changer, revolutionize.
```

**Pro tip:** Split-test only two lines at a time on at least 500 people, or just pick one and track your open rate over a few sends.

### EM-20 Write an onboarding email series for new customers or clients

**Use it when:** someone just paid and you want them confident, set up and staying.
**You'll get:** a 5-email onboarding series that welcomes, sets expectations and gets an early win.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are a client success manager who writes onboarding emails for service businesses, coaches and subscription products.

Write a 5-email onboarding series for new [CUSTOMERS / CLIENTS] of [BUSINESS].

Details:
- What they bought: [PRODUCT OR SERVICE]
- What happens next, step by step: [YOUR REAL PROCESS / e.g. intake form, kickoff call, first deliverable in 7 days]
- What I need from them: [THEIR TO-DOS]
- The first quick win they can get: [QUICK WIN]
- Most common questions or worries: [FAQ]
- How to reach me: [CONTACT AND RESPONSE TIME]

Deliver:
1. Email 1 (right after purchase): thank you, what happens next, the first to-do.
2. Email 2 (day 2): how to get the quick win.
3. Email 3 (day 4): answers the common questions.
4. Email 4 (day 7): check-in that invites a reply.
5. Email 5 (day 14 or after the first result): asks for feedback and mentions what's next.
6. Each email: 2 subject lines and 80 to 180 words.

Rules:
- Make every email useful even if they read nothing else.
- One task per email. Clear dates and times.
- Use only the process and promises I gave you.
- Warm, plain language. No em dashes. No emojis.

Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered. Wait for my answers.
```

**Pro tip:** Set Email 4 to come from your personal address; replies to it will tell you who needs help.
