# Offers & Sales

These prompts help you shape what you sell, price it, and turn interested people into paying clients. Every one keeps the claims honest, so you can use the output on real pages, calls and DMs without cleaning up made-up proof.

### OF-01 Build an irresistible offer for my service (asks questions first)

**Use it when:** people like what you do but don't buy fast, or they only compare you on price.
**You'll get:** a complete offer with a name, what's included, bonuses, a risk reducer and a one-line pitch.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are an offer strategist who helps local service businesses package what they do so customers stop haggling.

My business: [BUSINESS / e.g. mobile dog grooming in Tampa]
What I sell now and the price: [SERVICE AND CURRENT PRICE]
Who I want more of: [IDEAL CUSTOMER]

Before you write anything, ask me up to 7 questions about my customers, competitors and capacity. Wait for my answers.

Then give me:
1. The one painful problem this offer solves, in my customer's own words.
2. The outcome they want and a realistic timeframe.
3. The core offer: a plain name, what's included, the price.
4. 3 to 5 bonuses that remove effort or a known obstacle, each with one line on why it matters.
5. A risk reducer I can actually honor.
6. A one-sentence offer statement for a page or a DM.

Rules:
- Talk about the customer's problem before the offer.
- Only include bonuses I can actually deliver.
- Don't invent results, reviews or statistics.
- No fake urgency or scarcity.
- Write like a person talking to one person. No jargon. No em dashes.
- Never use these words: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust, "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "take it to the next level".

Then tell me the weakest part of this offer and how to fix it.
```

**Pro tip:** Paste in 3 real things customers said to you before buying, and the offer will sound like them, not like a brochure.

### OF-02 Price my service with three package tiers

**Use it when:** you charge one flat price and suspect you're leaving money on the table.
**You'll get:** three packages with names, contents, prices and the reasoning behind each.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are a pricing consultant who sets up Good, Better, Best packages for small service businesses and freelancers.

My service: [SERVICE / e.g. social media management for restaurants]
My current price: [PRICE]
My costs and time per client: [HOURS AND COSTS PER MONTH]
What competitors charge, if I know: [COMPETITOR PRICES]
My ideal customer: [AUDIENCE]

Before you write anything, ask me up to 5 questions about my capacity and best clients. Wait for my answers.

Then give me:
1. Three tiers with a plain name, a one-line "best for" description, what's included and a price.
2. The profit per client for each tier, using my numbers. Show the math.
3. Why the middle tier should be the easy choice.
4. What to leave out of the lowest tier so it's still profitable.
5. One sentence I can say on a call to present the three options.

Rules:
- Use only the numbers I give you. If something is missing, ask or mark it [NEED THIS].
- Each tier must differ by outcome or support, not by filler extras.
- Don't price below my costs. Flag it if my current price already is.
- Write like a person talking to one person. No jargon. No em dashes.
- Never use these words: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust, "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "take it to the next level".
```

**Pro tip:** After you get the tiers, ask "What would make someone jump from the middle tier to the top one?" and add that to the top tier.

### OF-03 Write a full landing page for one offer, section by section

Price and what's included: [PRICE AND INCLUSIONS]

Main action: [CALL TO ACTION / e.g. book a time, buy now]

Write the page in these sections, labeled:

2. The problem, in the customer's words.
3. What changes after they buy.
4. What's included, as a short list with one benefit per item.

- No em dashes. No emojis.
```

**Pro tip:** Read the headline options out loud to a customer and use the one they ask a question about.

Write 10 FAQ entries:

```

**Pro tip:** Paste in the exact wording from 5 real customer messages, typos and all, for the most useful answers.

### OF-06 Write a discovery call script that doesn't feel pushy

**Use it when:** you have sales calls booked and want a clear flow that ends in a yes or an honest no.
**You'll get:** a 20 to 30 minute call script with questions, transitions and a calm close.

```text

Who books calls with me: [AUDIENCE]
How they usually find me: [LEAD SOURCE / e.g. Instagram, referrals, Google]
Call length: [MINUTES]

Write the script in these parts:
1. Opening (30 seconds): set the agenda and ask permission to ask questions.
2. Current situation: 4 questions.
3. The problem and what it's costing them: 4 questions, including one about money or time.
4. What they want instead: 3 questions.
5. What they've tried: 2 questions.
6. A short summary line I say back to them.
7. How I present the offer, tied to what they told me (under 150 words).
8. A no-pressure close and what to say if they need time.
9. 3 signs this person is not a fit, and a polite way to say so.

Rules:
- The prospect should talk about 70 percent of the call.
- No fake urgency, no "this price is only for today" unless it's true.
- Don't promise results I didn't mention. Don't invent statistics.
- Write it the way people speak. No jargon. No em dashes.
- Never use these words: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust, "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "take it to the next level".
```

**Pro tip:** Print the questions on one page and tick them off during the call instead of reading the script word for word.
