# Ads

These 20 prompts take you from "I need an ad" to ads, hooks, scripts and landing pages you can launch on Meta or Google this week. Fill in the blanks in square brackets, paste the prompt into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and use the pro tip to make the result sharper.

### AD-01 Write 3 Facebook and Instagram ads from one offer

**Use it when:** you have one offer and need ready-to-run ads that each try a different angle.
**You'll get:** three complete Meta ads (pain, result and proof angles), each with primary text, headline and button.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are a direct-response copywriter who has written Facebook and Instagram ads for small businesses for 10 years.

Business: [BUSINESS / e.g. Smith Plumbing in Tulsa]
Offer: [OFFER / e.g. $49 drain cleaning this month]
Audience: [AUDIENCE]
Proof: [A REAL RESULT, REVIEW OR NUMBER, if you have one]

Write 3 ads, one per angle:
1. Pain: open with the customer's problem right now.
2. Result: open with life after the job is done.
3. Proof: open with the real proof above, or a specific detail about how we work if I gave none.

For each ad give me:
- Primary text, 60 to 125 words. The first line must work alone, since Meta hides the rest behind "See more".
- Headline, under 40 characters.
- Button: pick one of Learn More, Book Now, Get Quote, Sign Up, Shop Now.

Rules:
- Talk about the customer's problem before the offer.
- Use specific numbers, places and details from what I gave you. Don't invent statistics, reviews or results.
- No "you" statements about personal attributes (health, money, body, age). Meta rejects those.
- Write like a person talking to one person. Short sentences. No jargon.
- Never use these words: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust, "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "take it to the next level".
- No em dashes. No hashtags. No emojis.

Then tell me which one you'd test first and why.
```

**Pro tip:** Run all three as separate ads in one ad set, so Meta shows you which angle your audience cares about.

### AD-02 Generate 30 scroll-stopping hooks for my niche, ranked

**Use it when:** your ads get skipped and you need stronger first lines for copy or video.

**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
Business: [BUSINESS]
Niche: [NICHE / e.g. med spa in Scottsdale]
Customer: [AUDIENCE]
Main problem they have: [PROBLEM]
Offer: [OFFER]

Write 30 hooks, 5 in each of these 6 styles:
1. Call out the exact person
2. Surprising specific fact about the problem (only facts I gave you or common knowledge)
3. A mistake people make
4. A question they would answer "yes" to
5. A short story opening
6. A blunt opinion about the industry

Each hook: 12 words or fewer, works as the first line of an ad or the first 3 seconds of a video.

Then score every hook from 1 to 10 on how likely it is to stop this specific customer scrolling. Show a table ranked from highest to lowest: rank, hook, style, score.

Rules:
- Use specific numbers, places and details from what I gave you. Don't invent statistics or results.
- No clickbait the ad can't pay off. The hook must match the offer.
- No "you" statements about personal attributes (health, money, body, age).
- Never use: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, robust.
- No em dashes. No emojis.

Then explain why your top 5 win.
```

**Pro tip:** Send "Write 10 more in the style of my top 3" to go deeper on what already works.

### AD-03 Find my audience's real pain points before I write an ad

**Use it when:** you are not sure what your customers actually worry about, so your ads sound generic.
**You'll get:** a short interview, then a pain-point map in your customers' own words with ad angles for each.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are a customer researcher who helps small businesses find the words customers use before any ad gets written.

Business: [BUSINESS]
What I sell: [PRODUCT OR SERVICE]
Who buys it: [AUDIENCE, as best I know]
Things customers have said to me (optional): [PASTE REVIEWS, EMAILS, DMs OR CALL NOTES]

Before you write anything, ask me up to 7 questions about my customers: what they tried before, what made them call, what they complain about, what almost stopped them buying. Ask them one batch at a time and wait for my answers.

After I answer, give me:
1. The top 5 pain points, ranked by how often or how strongly they came up.
2. For each pain point: the exact phrases a customer would use (from my answers where possible), what they fear will happen if they do nothing, and what they have already tried.
3. The top 3 desires (what they want, not what they want to avoid).
4. 3 objections that stop them buying, with an honest answer to each.
5. 5 ad angles, each one sentence, built on the pain points above.

Rules:
- Quote my customers' words when I gave them. Mark anything you are guessing as "guess".
- Don't invent statistics, reviews or results.
- Plain language. No marketing jargon.
- No em dashes.
```

**Pro tip:** Paste in 5 to 10 real reviews, even ones about competitors, and the language you get back gets much sharper.

### AD-06 Write a Meta lead form ad plus questions that filter bad leads

**Use it when:** you run lead form ads and get too many people who never answer the phone or can't afford you.
**You'll get:** 2 lead ads, the form intro, 3 to 4 qualifying questions and a thank-you screen.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text

Offer in the ad: [OFFER / e.g. free in-home estimate]

My ideal lead: [IDEAL LEAD / e.g. homeowner, within 25 miles, ready in the next 3 months]
Leads I don't want: [BAD LEADS / e.g. renters, people just browsing prices]

Give me:
1. 2 ad versions: primary text (50 to 100 words), headline (under 40 characters). State the price range or starting price honestly so the wrong people skip it.
2. Form intro headline (under 60 characters) and 2 to 3 short lines on what happens after they submit.
3. 3 to 4 qualifying questions, multiple choice, that sort good leads from bad ones. For each, list the answer options and mark which answers mean a good lead.

```

**Pro tip:** A slightly harder form means fewer leads but more who pick up, so judge it on booked jobs, not lead count.

### AD-08 Write a 15 and 30 second video ad script with shot list

**Use it when:** you want to film a video ad yourself with a phone and need a script that fits the time.
**You'll get:** a 15-second and a 30-second script with timings, on-screen text and a shot list.
**Works in:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

```text
You are a video ad producer who scripts short phone-filmed ads for small businesses on Facebook, Instagram and Reels.

Business: [BUSINESS]
Offer: [OFFER]
Audience: [AUDIENCE]
Who will be on camera: [PERSON / e.g. me, the owner, or no person, just product and voiceover]
Where we can film: [LOCATIONS / e.g. shop floor, customer's kitchen, van]

Write 2 scripts:
1. 15 seconds (about 35 to 40 spoken words).
2. 30 seconds (about 70 to 80 spoken words).

Format each as a table with columns: time (e.g. 0 to 3s), what we see (shot), what is said, on-screen text.

Structure for both:
- 0 to 3 seconds: a hook that works with the sound off (strong visual plus on-screen text).
- Middle: the problem, then how we solve it, with one specific detail.

Then give me:
- A shot list: every shot numbered, with framing (close-up, wide, over-the-shoulder) and a note on what to film.

- Don't invent statistics, reviews or results.
- Spoken lines must sound natural out loud. Short sentences.
- No em dashes. No emojis.
```

**Pro tip:** Film the 3 alternative hooks too and swap only the first 3 seconds, which is the cheapest video test you can run.

### AD-13 Rewrite an ad that isn't working

**Use it when:** an ad has been running and the results are poor, and you want to know why before you spend more.
