# The seven readers

Chosen named these. Every email is written to exactly one of them. Before writing,
fill in the row for the reader you chose — the enemy, the demon, the future state, and
the jugular objection are what the copy is made of.

The frameworks behind the columns are from Chosen's notes: **attack their enemies**
(the struggle they're in — a rival, a setback, a doubting family, a bad belief),
**defeat their demons** (the cognitive-dissonance rationalization keeping them stuck:
"I'm not smart enough," "I don't care about that"), **forgive their faults** (let them
off the hook for not knowing — you're the lighthouse), and **the better future state**
(sales happen when the future is visibly better). The **jugular objection** is the one
the subject line raises that the body must kill.

## Student side — the Eat 4 Life list

### S-A · Just found out digital careers exist
- **Where they are:** working a job, scrolling, saw a clip or a post. "Wait, people get
  paid for this?" No vocabulary yet.
- **Enemy:** the job. The schedule that isn't theirs. The paycheck that's spoken for
  before it clears.
- **Demon:** "That's for tech people / people with a degree / people who already know
  somebody."
- **Forgive:** nobody told them. School didn't. Their parents couldn't.
- **Future state:** a second income they control, from their phone, this month.
- **Jugular objection:** "I don't know how to do anything."
- **Wants to hear:** you don't need to code, you need to know how to ask. One thing,
  tonight. A real example of a person like them who did it.
- **Angles that land:** Best Way Without · Paradigm Shift ("AI skills ≠ coding") ·
  Lifestyle.

### S-B · Knows nothing, got interested off Chosen's story
- **Where they are:** they've read or watched Chosen. They believe *him*; they don't
  yet believe *themselves*. Bought or is about to buy the book.
- **Enemy:** their own doubt, and the people around them who say "be realistic."
- **Demon:** "It worked for him because he was already a pro gamer / already had
  something." (Kill this with: pizza shop on Melrose, brand-ambassador gigs, no degree.)
- **Forgive:** wanting a guarantee before starting. Everyone does.
- **Future state:** the first paid thing. Not a business — the first $200.
- **Jugular objection:** "What do I actually do first?"
- **Wants to hear:** the origin story with the boring parts left in; the 90-day plan;
  "one role, one client."
- **Angles:** Story (origin) · Miyagi · Results (patient zero).

### S-C · Tried and failed
- **Where they are:** bought a course once, watched half of it, made $0, feels stupid
  and a little burned. The most valuable reader on the list if you don't shame them.
- **Enemy:** the last guru. The course that sold the dream and delivered a Notion
  template. Their own memory of quitting.
- **Demon:** "I'm just not the type who follows through." / "This stuff doesn't
  actually work, it's all upsells."
- **Forgive:** explicitly. The failure was the system, not them. Most courses have an
  8% completion rate — that's a design flaw, not a character flaw.
- **Future state:** finishing one thing. Proof to themselves.
- **Jugular objection:** "Why would this time be different?"
- **Wants to hear:** tilt control — every no is a lost round, queue the next one. The
  Worst Way (what they were sold). Consumption, not more content.
- **Angles:** Worst Way · Paradigm Shift · Story (Chosen's own failed attempts).

### S-D · Tried and succeeded, wants more
- **Where they are:** made money. A client, a gig, a few hundred a month. Now bored,
  or capped, or doing it all by hand.
- **Enemy:** the ceiling. Trading hours for dollars again, just freelance this time.
  (FEAST's "feast or famine trap.")
- **Demon:** "I need more clients." (No — better clients, positioned as growth, not a
  vendor.)
- **Forgive:** underpricing. Everyone starts there.
- **Future state:** fewer, better clients paying for outcomes. A system instead of a
  hustle. The five jobs, run for other people.
- **Jugular objection:** "I already know this."
- **Wants to hear:** positioning (sell the lead machine, not the website); the math of
  worth (10% of the growth you cause); recurring over one-off.
- **Angles:** 80/20 · Paradigm Shift · Results · Lifestyle.

## Agency side — the E4L Services list

### A-1 · Small business that wants more sales
- **Where they are:** a real business — HVAC, roofing, dental, med spa, chiro,
  grooming. Busy. Marketing is a thing they know they should do more of. Phone rings
  but not enough.
- **Enemy:** the slow month. The competitor with the nicer website. Yelp. The lead
  that called and nobody followed up.
- **Demon:** "I need more leads." (Usually they need follow-up — the leads they have
  are dying in a spreadsheet.)
- **Forgive:** running two of the five jobs. Everybody does. That's why they're busy.
- **Future state:** the phone rings *and* somebody answers; five bookings become
  fifteen without an extra ad dollar.
- **Jugular objection:** "I don't have time for this."
- **Wants to hear:** what this means for THEIR business, in their vertical, with a
  number. The four-minute test they can run today.
- **Angles:** Correlation (news → their business) · Best Way Without · Results ·
  Worst Way (the follow-up they're not doing).

### A-2 · Killing it, always adding to the bottom line
- **Where they are:** growing, profitable, hires people. Reads fast. Will pay for
  leverage; won't pay for a pitch.
- **Enemy:** the ceiling of their own time. The thing they know they should test but
  haven't. The competitor who gets there first.
- **Demon:** "We're fine." (Fine is where the leak is — retention, the customers
  quietly bleeding out while they chase new ones.)
- **Forgive:** nothing — respect them. Talk peer to peer.
- **Future state:** the edge. Being early. The next 20% with the same team.
- **Jugular objection:** "Why you and not my current guy?"
- **Wants to hear:** specific, dated, sourced. What shipped this week and what it
  unlocks. An opinion, not a summary.
- **Angles:** Paradigm Shift · 80/20 · Correlation (dated news) · Results with real
  numbers.

### A-3 · The confused entrepreneur — great at sales, bad at tools
- **Where they are:** can close anybody in a room. Has bought GHL, HubSpot, three
  Zaps, and a course, and none of it runs. Embarrassed about it.
- **Enemy:** the tool. The dashboard. The 47-step setup video. Every system that
  promised "easy."
- **Demon:** "I'm not a tech person." / "I'll set it up when things slow down." (They
  never do.)
- **Forgive:** hard and early. The tools *are* badly designed. Not knowing how to wire
  a workflow says nothing about whether they can run a business — they already run
  one.
- **Future state:** it just runs. Follow-up happens while they're in the room closing.
  Somebody else holds the tools.
- **Jugular objection:** "I've paid for this before and it sat there."
- **Wants to hear:** you bring the sales, we bring the system. Done-with-you.
  "AI Marketing Made Easy" is literally for them.
- **Angles:** Best Way Without (without learning the software) · Worst Way (the
  half-built stack) · Story.

## The 4% rule

Chosen's note: 4% of customers make 68% of sales. On both lists the 4% are S-D and
A-2 — the ones already winning. Don't write *down* to the whole list to reach the
newest reader; write to the 4% and let the rest overhear. Newcomers aspire up. Winners
don't read down.
