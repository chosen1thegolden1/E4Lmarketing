# The sauce — formulas that already work

Chosen's rule: use headlines that already work, swap the keywords. Practice on
yourself — if it works on you, it works.

## Subject lines

### Default: Curiosity + Benefit (JFSI)
Benefit + a curiosity hook. "THIS habit will boost your score" beat "How to overcome
procrastination without willpower" by +445% clicks. "#1 mistake that damages your
credit" beat "The #1 reason to have a credit card" by +323%.

Hooks that carry: THIS one thing · the #1 mistake · the weird trick · what [winners]
do before 8am · the worst way to · never do this · the real reason.

### Pattern interrupts — Billy Gene's 30 (one in five sends, not every send)
These win by not looking like marketing. Overuse and they become marketing.

Oops! You forgot something… · [Name], I'm a bit concerned · [Name]'s final notice ·
If I lose it all tomorrow… · I'll skip the BS · I know one of these is you (last
chance) · Billy wanted me to give you this link · Still looking? · Believe it or
not… this is what you need to do · I'll take the blame… · Can you take a look at
this · I know it's Friday but… you need to read this real quick · [Name]… did you
hear what happened yesterday?! · Your time off has been approved! · Your 50% off
coupon expires today · Hey stranger · Question · Disappointed · Your ad is approved ·
[Someone] commented on your link · Did we just become best friends? · Here's your
backstage pass… · Found wallet · Not gonna waste time with a BS subject line… just
read this, please · Should I unsubscribe you? · Reminder: you have a $10 credit ·
Your complimentary tickets have arrived! · Uh oh, did you miss it? · Hi, do you
remember me? · Will you marry me?

The E4L-safe ones: Question · Hey stranger · Still looking? · Should I unsubscribe
you? · I'll skip the BS · Can you take a look at this · Did you hear what happened
yesterday · Uh oh, did you miss it? · Hi, do you remember me? Skip the fake-notification
ones ("Your ad is approved") — they burn trust on a list that's supposed to know you.

### Headline formulas — Razor-Sharp Direct Response (opt-ins, promo emails)
- Why you don't need [unpleasant action] to get [result]
- How to [impossible goal] in [short time] without [the thing they think they need]
- [N] warning signs you're about to [bad result] (and how to avoid it)
- The big lie [experts] tell about [topic] that could [harm you]
- [N] mistakes you don't know you're making that cost you [money/outcome]
- [N] shocking habits of highly successful [audience]
- The truth about [topic] nobody wants you to read
- [Topic] expert reveals their [N]-step formula for [result] quickly and easily
- WARNING: do NOT [action] before you read this
- [Get benefit] in [short time] using these proven [topic] strategies
- What [audience] must know if they want to [result]
- The breakthrough method [N] [people] have used to [result] in [time]
- Find out how you can [eliminate biggest problem] and [get benefit]
- **The one that always works:** Finally! How to get [result] without [thing they fear
  most] in [timeframe] — guaranteed.

Sub-headline restates the offer *specifically* and says exactly how it arrives.

### Bullets that make people click
- How to X without Y — "washboard abs without a single sit-up"
- You need X, right? WRONG. — address a belief, then talk against it
- X ways to Y — the classic
- Where to find Y — "where to find the hyperactive buyers"
- How to eliminate X — "stop joint pain forever"
- What you should never — fear of loss beats fear of missing gain
- Say goodbye to [frustration]
- The truth about Y — "the dirty truth about…"
- Have you been doing X wrong? (And does it matter?)

## Sabri Suby's 7-part follow-up — the escalation cadence

For anyone who raised a hand (booked nothing, downloaded the report, opted in, hit
the scorecard). Each email is more direct than the last. Map onto GHL as the
abandoned-action / welcome sequence.

1. **Did you get this?** — check-in on the thing they grabbed; first ask for the
   call, no pressure, "few slots this month."
2. **[Big benefit] in 30 mins / Can you hop on the phone?** — "I have X sessions
   left"; three fascination bullets about what they'll learn; "do you want one?"
3. **Got questions? / Escape the curse of knowledge** — more info made it *more*
   confusing; experience is the way out; here's mine; free consult.
4. **Hundreds of [people] can't be wrong** — social proof, one testimonial; "I like to
   make sure we're a fit"; the discovery call gives one real secret.
5. **I'm confused… / Frankly, I'm a little surprised** — "you still haven't taken me
   up"; "if you really want X (and I'm guessing you do or you wouldn't be here)."
6. **Don't miss this / Need to let this go** — "X spots left, gone by Friday"; "if
   you're truly, sincerely serious."
7. **Last chance… / I need a decision** — "I've held a spot for a week; end of today
   I release it"; "do nothing and it goes to someone else"; "what's it to be?"

The rhythm that makes it aggressive without being sleazy: every email has a real
reason to exist (a report, a spot, a deadline), and the tone stays warm while the ask
gets sharper.

## FEAST — Sean Vosler's positioning (for the agency side and S-D)

- You're not selling a website; you're selling a lead machine. Not ads — eyeballs
  that buy. Not coaching — confidence. **What they're buying is the end result, not
  the thing.**
- Good clients don't care what you do; they care what it does to their bottom line.
  Everything you do traces to more sales or less risk. Say that.
- Competition is thinner at the top. Most agencies fight for the middle because they
  think they're not qualified for bigger clients.
- Worth = the outcome you cause. Double a $1M business and 10% of the growth is
  $100K. Do the math out loud in the email.
- Sell the right thing to the right person and you won't have to sell. Identify who
  the math makes sense for and pursue *them*.
- "The client's not stupid, they're just not informed. Yet." — educate, then the
  price is easy.

## Chosen's notes — the parts that are rules

- Think of their needs. You have the length of a hug to grab attention.
- Subject line = 80 cents of the dollar. It raises the jugular objection; the body
  kills it.
- Meet them on their journey. Find where the rubber meets the road.
- **Fan their flames.** Use what's already working for them. Sails, not oars. People
  want to learn, feel right, and feel like they got a deal. Hope is the strongest fan.
- Fear only with the exit: "you have it, but now that you know, here's what you do."
- **Logic is panic's prey.** Restate the value, give examples of use.
- **Forgive their faults** in the copy *and* the CTA. Be the lighthouse. Write
  half-full even when they see half-empty.
- Relate, relate, relate. They have to see themselves in it or they start biased.
- **Attack their enemies.** List features → the struggle each one fights → how it
  felt → rank. Turn local tension universal. Make the enemy a boogeyman. "The problem
  with [common belief] isn't Y, it's Z."
- The three minds: open, closed, and ideological (sees new ideas as a threat). Crack
  the third with questions, not statements.
- Bring their day into it. Where they read, what they fear, how they spend it.
- The perfect email: ~135 words, line break every one or two sentences.
- The frame decides the choice. "200 saved" and "400 die" are the same numbers.
- **The benefits do the selling.** Not the copy, not the writer, not the letter.
- IKEA / endowment: value is created *after* the sale. Consumption emails matter.
- Expert, not friend.
