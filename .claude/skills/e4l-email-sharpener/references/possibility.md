# Possibility emails — study notes for the writer and the editor

**What this is.** Study material, not a schedule. Chosen, 2026-10-31: *"We don't need to do
anything special for the 12 months. Only mark down these things as things for the 2 loops
to study."* Nothing here forces a slot. The writer reaches for it when a student email
should sell the *dream* of making money with AI. The editor uses the walls at the bottom
when it's editing one.

**Why they matter.** Chosen, a former Billy Gene student, on what excites and motivates:
*"the promise of making money and using these tools to continue making money or break
away from the rat race. Just like we talk about in Xbox to Exec."* And on the mix:
*"Mixing emails like this in with the sales emails and trending topic updates that still
give them these pillars as their northern star will garner the most interest around the
courses."*

**Chosen's verdict on the five examples (2026-10-31):** *"these are 8/10 the energy we want
to get them to take action… they should guide the point. No matter what product we drop."*
So the energy is the bar for every student email, not just this shape.

**Shape:** Shape 2 in `templates.md`. **Five finished examples:**
`docs/emails/STUDENT_POSSIBILITY_SERIES.md` (image, website, game, video, creatives). Read
one before writing another. Their energy is the target.

## The five pillars (every possibility email carries all five)

1. **Possibility:** one thing you can make with AI, shown as a scene they can go check
   tonight, not a feature list.
2. **Ease:** a phone, one sentence, a few minutes. Quote the actual prompt.
3. **Money:** what a business really pays **E4L** for that work, from e4lagency.com that
   week, then "That's ONE client." Never what the reader will earn.
4. **Lifestyle:** from anywhere, around the thing you love, out of the rat race. Shown
   through Chosen's real story and the real team, never promised.
5. **Belief + timing:** Chosen believes they can do it, said plainly and varied. And the
   door is open *now*, as opinion, never a fake deadline.

## Walls: the editor enforces these too

- **No income claims.** No "you'll make," no "per client per month," no "replace your
  salary." Our prices, a real proof (story bank, the Drip screenshot), and the reader's own
  math. An earnings promise to course buyers is what the FTC goes after.
- **Tool claims stay modest and sourced the week they're written.** "A simple game." "A
  one-page site." "A video ad in minutes."
- **Scenes are invitations, not events.** "Picture it" and "try this tonight" are fine.
  "A student of mine last week…" only if Chosen supplied it.
- **CTA is the book** until Chosen names another student front end.
