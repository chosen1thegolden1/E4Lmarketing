# The operating system — Just F*cking Send It (Ian Stanley, 2025)

Chosen's own distillation of the book is the source; this is the working version.
Four principles, then the frameworks you'll actually reach for while writing.

**Send more. Send better. Send the right emails at the right time. Don't be boring.**

## Why the fear is wrong (Ch. 1–2)

- A list you're afraid to email is worth pennies. They signed up to hear from you
  regularly, "not once a month when Mercury is in retrograde."
- Conventional: $1/subscriber/month. Ian's: $5–15. Sending once a month out of fear
  makes ~10% of what the list could. Perfectionism has a dollar cost.
- Floor: one a week. Realistic: 3–6 (M/W/F is fine). Ceiling: two a day, only closing
  a promo, only to people who opened in the last 7 days.
- **Consistency beats frequency.** Three a week for months > daily for five days then
  eight days of silence. Inconsistency is how you get forgotten.
- **Sunday is the highest-sales day.** People decide to change on Sunday — dreading
  Monday, ashamed of the weekend. Nobody starts a diet on Saturday.
- Unsubscribes: don't track them. Track opens, clicks, sales. Someone who leaves was
  never going to buy. If they leave because the email sucked, fix that.
- Target 20%+ opens (millions are made at 20%); 25–35% keeps deliverability healthy.

## Don't be boring · Be vain (Ch. 3–4)

Better to be different than better. Zig when they zag. Double down on quirks; take
a stand; be polarizing; use your real language. **Connection happens around the rough
edges** — the struggles, the behind-the-scenes, the elevator fart. Treat the list like
a diary that makes money — *and always tie it back to a lesson for them.* Test: a
stranger asks how your dog is doing and you realize you told them in an email.

## Email Core Offer Wheel — "what do I promote today?" (Ch. 5)

Four spokes; rotate them. One spoke per email.

| Spoke | What it is | E4L student | E4L agency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low Ticket Front End | Under $100. The buyer generator. Make it *good* — $5 buyers become $5,000 buyers | The book (free + $9.95 shipping) | The scorecard / free report |
| High Ticket Backend | $1K–5K+. The profit center | The course / cohort | The retainer packages |
| Recurring Income | Monthly. Covers the business | Membership (if/when) | The monthly retainer itself |
| Promos + Stories | One offer, one deadline, a **reason why** it exists and why it ends. 1–2 promo weeks a month; stories every week, P.S. selling a spoke | | |

The center of the wheel is your north star — for E4L it's the retainer on the agency
side and the course on the student side. Most emails point at the spoke that feeds it.

**Know your list.** The #1 not-so-secret secret. A mediocre email about the exact
thing they want beats a great email about something adjacent. Content > skill.

## Emotional Bank Account (Ch. 6)

Every email is a deposit, neutral, or withdrawal.

- **Deposit** (Positive Emotional Reaction): a nose-breath laugh, something learned, a
  belief they hold confirmed ("sharing beliefs is a bonding experience in a divided
  world"), a paradigm shifted, a great story, a motivation.
- **Neutral**: they felt nothing. "Emotionless emails are forgotten." Never the goal.
- **Withdrawal** (Negative): a pure pitch with no value; a three-paragraph "5 hours
  left." For some readers that's a deposit — they wanted the reminder.

You withdraw because you run a business. Keep the balance positive so you can keep
withdrawing. This is the whole difference between a list that pays for a decade and
one that churns in two months.

## Just The Tip (Ch. 7)

One email, one thing. One tip, idea, outcome, taken *just* far enough. Three kinds of
sender: the **Used Car Salesman** (always pitching, churns), the **Teacher** (gives it
all away, trains the list not to buy), the **Speaker** (entertains *and* teaches,
leaves them wanting the rest). Be the Speaker. Every list is trained by its owner —
train them to click and they click; train them to expect free and they never buy.

## Subject lines (Ch. 8)

- Goal: make them obsolete — they open because it's *you*. Until then:
- **Curiosity + Benefit** almost always wins on opens, clicks *and* sales, and doesn't
  wear out as long as the body delivers both. Benefit ("make more sales") + curiosity
  hook ("THIS habit," "the #1 mistake," "the weird trick," "what successful people do").
- Four more that test: How-To Direct Benefit · Shock and Awe ("I made $55K from 200
  people") · Personal Story · The Question ("Are you making these 3 mistakes?").
- Test dramatically different things, judge on clicks not opens. Subject lines move
  clicks and sales more than opens.
- Clickbait the body doesn't fulfill destroys trust faster than anything.

## The angles — pick one per email (Ch. 9–15)

**Miyagi / soft teach.** Give the WHAT free, gate the HOW. Hook (unexpected
statement) → story or concept → core lesson → business application → CTA → casual
P.S. Teach through metaphor; state results with numbers; hold back implementation.
"You help people more by not giving away too much" — because the business survives.

**Correlation.** Explain something they already understand (a rock in a shoe,
seatbelts, sourdough) → relate it to their problem → the click is the resolution.
Brains crave connection; people value what they discover over what they're told.
Look *outside* your industry — the further the source, the stronger the click. Three
patterns: same principle, same process, same problem. Sweet spot: not obvious, then
undeniable. Sand down hammer transitions ("Now that you see X, here's how it hits
you") so the reader doesn't fall off the bridge.

**RVL — Results, Value, Lifestyle.** Results: case studies, before/afters; no
testimonials yet → *you* are patient zero. Value: real help inside the email, link in
the P.S. Lifestyle: "people don't buy products, they buy better versions of
themselves" — sell the sizzle, the thing underneath the thing.

**Story.** The whole game is **tension** — build it, hold it, release it at the last
possible moment. The stories that scare you to tell connect most. Hero story (8
beats): had a problem → how bad it was → almost gave up → what hit you → worked for
you → so you built a system → why it'll work for them → the alternative is all your
failures, or the shortcut. Origin story if no hero story: how did Bruce Wayne become
Batman. Keep a notes-app of every mildly interesting thing — Seinfeld emails come from
there.

**Best Way Without.** Indy shoots the sword guy. Find what they hate about the
conventional path (slow, embarrassing, expensive, technical) and sell the WITHOUT.
"Six-pack abs without giving up pizza" pulled 3–4× the clicks of the exercise list.
Structure: the painful old way → your WITHOUT → why the old way isn't necessary → a
taste → proof → the ask.

**Worst Way.** Blockbuster laughing at Netflix. Loss aversion: people do more to avoid
losing $100 than to gain $1,000. "The worst way to build a list (99% do this)" beats
"5 steps to build a list." The flaw has to be *fundamental* — "your whole strategy is
Blockbuster" — not a font choice. Make it visceral, build the contrast, offer the
escape hatch.

**Paradigm Shift.** Crack their matrix. "The reason you can't lose weight isn't that
you eat too much — it's that you eat too little." Must be genuinely insightful,
defensible, and more useful than the old belief. Collision → expose flaws → evidence
from sources they trust → the new reality → your solution built for it. The feeling:
"the thing I thought was helping me was hurting me."

**80/20 / the putting green.** Everyone wants to drive farther; the putting green
wins. Show them WHAT the big wins are, gate the HOW. Open loop to the next email.

**FAQ — the last-day email (Ch. 17).** Gather real questions ("Questions?" as a P.S.).
Bold each question, answer under it, **a link under every answer**. Send the last day
of a promo. "A lot of people are one question away from buying."

## The five sequences every business needs (Ch. 18)

1. **Welcome** — 5–7 emails, daily, at peak interest. Value, then an offer *early*
   (by email 2). Hero/origin story in the first few. Then to broadcasts.
2. **Nurture / ascension** — optional, longer. Best stories, proof, the higher offer.
3. **Abandoned action** — reached checkout or the application and stopped. 3–5 emails
   on the hesitations. Alone, this is +30% sales for almost any business.
4. **Re-engagement** — the nine-word email: *"Are you still interested in [thing]?"*
   That's the whole email. Automate at 90 days of no opens. Half of inquirers buy
   within 18 months; only 15% in the first 90 days.
5. **Post-purchase consumption** — where 95% fail. Help them *use* it. Course
   completion goes from 8% to 80–90%. The best time to sell is right after they've
   used what they bought.

Sequence rules: lead with value but sell early · micro-commitments (reply, click) ·
one great story · urgency increases as it goes · open loops between emails · one
offer at a time, built to as the natural next step.

## Calls to action (Ch. 19)

The CTA is the pivot point of the hero's journey — the red pill, the lightsaber. Most
emails "lead absolutely nowhere." Bottom of the email or the P.S.; one link, usually.
Early links get clicks but not the right clicks — the email sets up the click.

Six that actually get used: "Click here" (75% of the time; if it works, don't fix it)
· link text = subject line (congruent with why they opened) · **reply to this email**
(gauges interest *and* tells the inbox providers people want you — deliverability) ·
the P.S. (used in half of all emails; reads as a soft pitch, sells more) · a
hyperlinked domain under the signature (few clicks, profitable ones) · "3 ways to work
with me" (a P.S. listing three offers, priciest first).

## The fourteen checks — run before it goes in the doc

If it passes ten, send it.

1. Congruent with why they joined the list?
2. Would I want to read this? At least a nose-breath laugh?
3. Some "me" in it — tied back to a lesson for them?
4. Which spoke am I promoting: front end, backend, recurring, or a promo?
5. Deposit or withdrawal? Did I deliver value, entertainment, or something great?
6. ONE tip? Am I the Speaker?
7. Subject: Curiosity + Benefit? Does the body keep the promise?
8. Gave the WHAT, gated the HOW?
9. Any hammer transitions to sand down?
10. If a story: held the tension to the end? True?
11. CTA at the bottom, one link, natural next step?
12. No link? Then a P.S., a signature domain, or a reply ask.
13. Promo: reason why, deadline, FAQ on the last day?
14. Took 7–20 minutes? Is the next one on the calendar?
