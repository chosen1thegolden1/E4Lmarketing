# The season — US holidays, and what they do to the reader

**Chosen's instruction (2026-10-31):** both lists are mostly American. Be aware of the
holidays, tie them to sales, offers and what's interesting about that season, and use the
psychology of the moment to get the reader to act.

Read this **before** picking the angle, every week. The season is not decoration you add
at the end — it changes who the reader is that week, and a correct email sent into the
wrong week still loses.

## The rule that keeps this honest

A holiday is a reason the **reader's own situation** changed, not a costume for the copy.

- **Good:** "Your phone stops ringing the week of Thanksgiving. Here's what to do with
  those five days." That's true about their business and it's useful.
- **Bad:** "🦃 Gobble up these marketing tips!" Nobody has ever booked a call off that.
- **Forbidden:** inventing an offer or a deadline to borrow the season's urgency. If
  there is no real Black Friday offer, there is no Black Friday deadline. **A promo
  needs Chosen's decision — ask, never invent one.** This is the same wall as never
  inventing a fact.

Promo decisions live in `docs/PROMOS.md`. **If it is not written there, it does not
exist.** No live row means the batch ships with no promo and the writer asks him.

Seasonal urgency only works when the clock is real. Three kinds are always real and need
nobody's permission: **the calendar** (the year ends whether anyone buys or not), **their
own slow season** (the phone genuinely goes quiet), and **the cohort of people who
decided in January and were already running by March.**

### Send slots that collide, Nov 2026 to Jan 2027

Our slots are Mon / Wed / Fri / Sun at 8:00 AM Pacific. These land on or beside a
holiday, and each one is a decision rather than a default:

| Slot | Collides with | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Wed **Nov 11** | Veterans Day | Send. Don't do a Veterans Day tribute — it reads as borrowed. If you mention it at all, one honest line, no flag emoji, no offer attached. |
| Wed **Nov 25** | day before Thanksgiving | **Best slot of the month.** Inboxes are quiet, people are winding down and will read something short. Keep it short and end the week cleanly. |
| Fri **Nov 27** | Black Friday | Everyone's inbox is a war zone. Two honest options: a real offer, or the pattern interrupt of not selling. Don't send a normal value email into that noise and expect it to be read. |
| Sun **Nov 29** | day before Cyber Monday | Strong for the student list — the book is a low-ticket impulse buy and Sunday is already the highest-intent day. |
| Mon **Nov 30** | Cyber Monday | Same as Black Friday: real offer, or deliberately out of the fight. |
| Wed **Dec 23** | before Christmas Eve | Last useful send before the holiday. Make it the one that closes the year's argument. |
| Fri **Dec 25** | **Christmas Day** | **Do not send.** Nobody is reading, and arriving at 8am Christmas morning costs goodwill you can't buy back. Skip the slot or move it to Dec 23. |
| Wed **Dec 30** | before New Year's Eve | Good. People are already thinking about next year. |
| Fri **Jan 1** | **New Year's Day** | Don't send a normal broadcast. Either skip, or send one short thing that earns its place on that date. |

**Skipping a slot is allowed on these two dates and nowhere else.** Say it in the doc
header so nobody thinks the batch is short by accident.

## What each season does to each reader

### Late October → mid November: the year is closing
- **Agency (A-1, A-2, A-3):** Q4. They can see what the year did and it is too late to
  fix it with anything slow. Budget conversations are happening. The honest hook is: the
  phone slows down in December, and January's pipeline is built in November by whoever
  bothers. *Angles: Worst Way, Correlation, the direct one.*
- **Student (S-A, S-B, S-C, S-D):** holiday spending is coming and the money isn't
  there. This is the most financially motivated month of the year for someone who wants
  a second income, and it is honest to say so. *Angles: Best Way Without, Miyagi — one
  thing they can do this week for actual money.*

### Thanksgiving week
- **Agency:** work genuinely stops. Five days where nobody expects them to be at their
  desk is when an owner can finally look at the business instead of working in it. "The
  one thing to fix while nobody's calling."
- **Student:** family. Which means the question — "so what are you doing now?" — and for
  S-C, who tried and failed, that question stings. Handle that reader gently and
  specifically. It is the best week all year for the forgiveness angle.

### Black Friday / Cyber Monday
- **Agency:** they are *running* promos, not reading them. Talk about their offer, not
  ours. "Here's what your follow-up should say to everyone who bought this weekend" is
  genuinely useful on the Sunday or the Monday after.
- **Student:** they are *buying.* A $9.95 book is an easy yes in a week when spending
  feels normal. This is the single best week of the year for the front end.
- **Both:** if Chosen wants a real promo, it needs a reason why, one offer and a real
  deadline, per `jfsi.md`. **Ask him by the Tuesday before Nov 17** so the batch for the
  week of Nov 23 can be built around it. Without his word, no promo.

### December: the dead zone and the turn
- Weeks 1–2 are flat; nobody starts anything. Weeks 3–4 people stop pretending and start
  thinking about next year.
- **Agency:** the year-in-review email, honestly done, beats any tip. What worked, what
  didn't, what you're changing.
- **Student:** "this time next year" is the whole hook, and in December it isn't a cliché
  because they're already doing the arithmetic themselves.

### January: the only month people change
- The highest-intent month on both lists, and the most crowded. Everyone sends a
  new-year email, so the differentiator is being specific while everyone else is
  inspirational.
- **Agency:** budgets reset and the year is open. A Game Plan Call has a better reason to
  exist in January than any other month.
- **Student:** peak resolution energy, and peak quitting by February. Say that out loud.
  The reader who hears "most of the people reading this will have stopped by Valentine's
  Day, here's how not to be one of them" trusts you more than the one who gets cheered
  at.
- **MLK Day** is not a sales hook. Leave it alone unless Chosen has something real to say.

### February → April
- **Valentine's / Presidents Day:** retail noise, little meaning for either list. Skip
  unless a client's vertical cares.
- **Tax season to Apr 15:** strong for agency. Small owners are looking at last year's
  numbers whether they want to or not, which makes "where the money leaked" land harder
  in March than any other month.

### May → August
- **Memorial Day, July 4, Labor Day:** long weekends. Send Tuesday instead of Monday when
  Monday is the holiday, and say so in the header.
- **Summer:** slower for both lists. Good season for story and nurture, weak for hard
  asks. Build the Emotional Bank Account so autumn's asks have somewhere to draw from.

### September
- The real new year for business. Kids back at school, budgets re-examined, everyone
  suddenly serious. Treat September like January with less noise — it is the better month
  to ask for a call.

## Operating rules

1. **Name the season in the doc header** every week: which holidays fall in or beside the
   send week, and what the batch did about them. The Friday freshness check verifies it.
2. **One seasonal email a week, maximum.** Four in a row makes the list feel like a
   catalogue. The season sets the frame; it does not become the subject of every send.
3. **No holiday emoji as decoration.** 🦃🎄🎅 and the rest are instant unsubscribes in a
   business inbox. The emoji rules in `formatting.md` do not loosen because it's December.
4. **No "Happy [holiday]" subject lines.** They are the most-deleted subject line shape
   in the inbox.
5. **A real deadline or none.** Seasonal urgency is only honest when something actually
   ends. The calendar always ends; a sale only ends if Chosen said so.
6. **Both lists, different holidays.** The same date hits an owner and a job-hunter
   differently. Thanksgiving is quiet-time for one and a hard family question for the
   other. Never write one seasonal email and split it.
7. **Skip Christmas Day and New Year's Day.** Those are the only two slots it is right to
   skip, and the skip gets stated in the header.
