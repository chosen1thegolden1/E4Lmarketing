# E4L Email Sharpener

The goal Chosen set: **be the best email marketing company in the game inside 90 days.**
"Best" is measured by the Monday report — opens (once the GHL token can read them),
clicks, form fills, bookings, purchases, replies — not by how clever the copy feels.
Every rule below exists because it moves one of those numbers.

Everything here comes from seven sources Chosen chose, plus his own notes. The
references folder holds the detail; this file is the procedure. Read the reference
that matches the job before writing — it's five minutes and it's the difference between
sounding like Chosen and sounding like a copywriter doing Chosen.

## What "aggressive" means here

Chosen asked for aggressive emails on both sides. Aggressive is not loud, and it is not
sleazy — the sources are unanimous that a used-car-salesman list churns and burns.
Aggressive means:

- **Send more.** Three a week is the floor, not the ceiling. Daily during a promo week.
  An email that isn't sent makes $0.00. Consistency beats intensity — M/W/F held for
  months beats daily for a week then silence.
- **Sell in every email.** There is a link, at the bottom or in the P.S., in ~90% of
  emails. "Pussyfooting the call to action" is the #1 failure mode. If the reader has
  the problem today, making them wait five value emails before the offer is cowardice
  dressed as generosity.
- **Sell early in a sequence.** The welcome sequence makes its offer by email 2. People
  buy soonest after they join — that window is the most valuable one you'll ever have.
- **Take a stand.** Polarize. Attack the enemy by name. Say the thing the reader
  suspects but nobody in their feed will say. Better to be loved-or-hated than
  forgotten; the inbox is the easiest place on earth to be forgotten.
- **One tip, gated.** Give the WHAT free, charge for the HOW. Every email is one idea,
  taken just far enough that the reader wants the rest — and the rest is the link.
- **Escalate.** A follow-up sequence gets more direct with each email, ending in a
  decision demand ("I need your answer today"). See Sabri's 7-part in `sauce.md`.

And the balance that makes aggressive sustainable: the **Emotional Bank Account.** Every
email is a deposit (a laugh, a lesson, a confirmed belief, a story) or a withdrawal (a
pure pitch). Keep the balance positive and you can sell hard forever. Run it negative
and the list dies in sixty days. Most emails should deposit *and* sell — that's the
Speaker, not the Teacher, not the Used Car Salesman.

## The procedure

1. **Name the reader.** One of the seven segments in `audiences.md`. Not "students" —
   *which* student. The email is written to one person.
2. **Name the enemy and the demon.** What are they struggling against this week (the
   enemy: a bad job, a slow month, a tool they can't make work)? What false belief keeps
   them stuck (the demon: "I'm not technical," "I need more leads, not better
   follow-up")? The email attacks the enemy and cracks the demon. Chosen's note: turn
   the local tension into the universal one — "you're leaving thousands on the table
   monthly, and it's blocking the thing you actually want, because you don't know."
3. **Pick the angle.** From the JFSI rotation: Story · Results / Value / Lifestyle ·
   Miyagi (soft teach) · Correlation · Best Way Without · Worst Way · Paradigm Shift ·
   FAQ. Don't repeat last send's angle. The angle picks the structure; `jfsi.md` has
   each one's skeleton.
4. **Pick the wheel spoke.** What's being sold: the low-ticket front end (the book /
   the scorecard), the high-ticket backend (the course, the retainer), the recurring
   thing, or a promo with a deadline. One spoke per email.
5. **Write the subject last, but write it.** Curiosity + Benefit is the default and
   almost always wins on clicks and sales. Use `sauce.md` for pattern-interrupts
   (Billy Gene's list — one in five sends, not every send) and headline formulas.
   Chosen's rule: when the subject is written, 80 cents of the dollar is spent. The
   "jugular objection" the subject raises has to be answered in the body.
6. **Write it like a text to one person.** Chosen's note: the perfect email is ~135
   words with a line break every one or two sentences. Broadcasts can run to ~200 if
   the story needs it. Sequences run shorter. The nine-word re-engagement is nine words.
   Write like talking to a friend, include a specific number, hold back the
   implementation.
7. **Land the CTA.** Bottom of the email or in the P.S. One link. The natural next
   step of what you just said — never a bolted-on ad. Chosen's P.S. convention (a
   question they can answer in one line) doubles as a reply CTA, which also feeds
   deliverability. Both CTAs can coexist: link above the signature, question in the
   P.S.
8. **Run the fourteen checks** in `jfsi.md` before it goes in the doc. If it passes
   ten, send it. Good-enough-and-sent beats perfect-and-drafted.

## Hard walls — these don't move

- **Agency copy says "AI Marketing Made Easy."** Never "Everybody Eats." Student copy
  owns "Everybody Eats" and "Holla Back – Get ya Dolla Back. ✊🏾"
- **Agency CTA is the call. Student CTA is the book.** One CTA per email.
- **Gaming is the handshake, not the vocabulary.** The reader should finish knowing
  Sebastian came out of gaming and seeing their own relationship with it in his story —
  without needing to know a specific game. "Side quest," "still waiting on GTA 6," "when
  everybody unlocked the same gun" land on anybody. "Ranged Damage role" doesn't.
- **Student copy assumes zero experience.** No jargon, nothing that expects a portfolio
  or clients. If a normal person couldn't do it tonight on their phone, wrong side quest.
- **The book's five roles are translated in email, not quoted.** "Five jobs every
  business needs handled" — never "Damage / Support / Tank."
- **Never invent a fact.** Every stat is dated and sourced under the email. Every story
  is true — Hollywood-ize the telling, never the events.
- **Nothing sends from gsgagency.com.** That domain is still healing.
- **Every link carries UTMs and a `(LINK: …)` marker.** No marker, no attribution, and
  the Monday report can't see the sale. Format is in `assets/email-template.md`.

## Output

Deliver into the review doc format Zion and the formatter already use — see
`assets/email-template.md`. Subject, preview text, body with `(LINK: url?utm…)` markers,
a Notes line, a Source line. Batches go in `docs/BROADCAST_WEEK_<date>.md` and get
published as the Google Doc.

When asked to *review* rather than write: score against the fourteen checks, name the
segment and angle you think it's aimed at, and say which one thing would move clicks
most. Don't rewrite unless asked.
