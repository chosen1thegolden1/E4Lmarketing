# Role: Broadcast Email Formatter

Short-term contract role. One doc a week. Proofread, format, hyperlink, hand back.

**Where it sits in the chain:** Claude writes the copy → Chosen + Zion review the substance
→ **this person formats and proofs** → Daniel and Sammy load and send → Abdullah for GHL
support if needed.

They are the last set of eyes before the emails leave the doc. They are not a writer and
not a reviewer of ideas. They make finished copy *look* finished and make sure every link
works.

---

## What this actually costs

Eight emails a week. Roughly 10 minutes each for a careful pass, plus link checking.
Realistically **90 minutes to 2 hours a week** once they know the format — a little more
the first two weeks.

That's a freelancer on a small weekly retainer, not a hire. **Pay a flat weekly rate, not
hourly** — hourly on a 90-minute job invites padding, and the thing you actually want is
care, not time.

## Hire on the test, not the resume

Send finalists **one real email from a past week** with three problems planted in it:

1. **A typo** — a doubled word or a wrong homophone. Tests basic proofreading.
2. **A `(LINK: NEEDED — ...)` marker.** The right answer is to leave it and flag it.
   Someone who deletes it, or invents a plausible URL, will silently ship a dead CTA.
3. **A factual claim that's off** — a wrong date or a number that contradicts the source
   note underneath. The right answer is to comment on it, not fix it.

**The one that matters is #2.** Catching a typo is table stakes. Knowing to leave a
blocker visible rather than tidy it away is the whole job. Anyone who "helpfully" filled
it in is the wrong hire, no matter how clean the rest looks.

## Access

Give them **edit access to the week's doc only** — not the folder, not the archive.
Each week is a new file, so each week is a new share. That's slightly more clicking and
it means a bad week can't reach back through the history.

They never need GHL access. They never need the lead list. If they ask for either, that's
a conversation with Chosen, not a default.

# The SOP — give this to whoever gets hired

## Before you start

You'll get a link to one Google Doc each week. Everything you need is inside it.

**Work only inside the emails.** Each one starts with a line like `=== S1 — MONDAY
(STUDENT) ===` and ends at the `Source:` line. Everything above the first email and
everything below the last one is internal notes — leave all of it alone.

**Two sections are off limits entirely:**

- **The APPENDIX at the bottom.** Those are Chosen's original emails, kept as written on
  purpose. Do not touch them, even if you spot a typo. Especially if you spot a typo.
- **THE BOOK'S FRAMEWORK** section. Internal reference, never sent to anyone.

---

## The one rule that matters most

**You format and proofread. You do not rewrite.**

If a sentence is clunky, leave it. If a joke doesn't land for you, leave it. If a claim
looks wrong — a date, a number, a statistic — **leave a comment, don't fix it.** The copy
has already been reviewed by two people and every fact is sourced.

Fixing a typo is your job. Changing a word because you'd have said it differently is not.

When in doubt: **comment, don't edit.**

---

## Step 1 — Read it once, straight through

Before touching anything. You're reading for the stuff a spellchecker misses:

- Doubled words ("the the")
- Wrong homophone (their/there, your/you're)
- A sentence that just stops
- A name or a place that looks wrong
- Anything that made you stop and re-read it

Fix the mechanical ones. Comment the rest.

---

## Step 2 — Apply the formatting

Same spec on every email, every week. Consistency is the point.

| Part | Formatting |
|---|---|
| `Subject:` and `Preview text:` lines | **Leave plain.** They get typed into separate fields in the sending tool — formatting there does nothing. |
| The numbered beat headers — `1. Think of a business with no website.` | **Bold** the whole line |
| Copy-paste prompts (the blocks in quotes the reader is meant to copy) | *Italic*, and indent one level. They should look obviously separate from the rest. |
| The single sharpest line in the email | **Bold** — one per email, not three. If you bold four things, you've bolded nothing. |
| The `👉` CTA line | Leave the text as-is, just hyperlink it (Step 3) |
| Everything else | Plain |

Emoji stay exactly where they are. Don't add any. Don't remove any.

---

## Step 3 — The links

Throughout the emails you'll see lines like:

```
Read the launch → (LINK: https://example.com/the-story)
```

For each one:

1. Select the visible text — `Read the launch →`
2. Hyperlink it to the URL inside the marker
3. **Delete the `(LINK: ...)` part entirely.** The reader must never see it.
4. **Click the link and confirm the page opens.** Every single one, every week. A link
   that 404s is worse than no link.

### The exception — do not clean this one up

If a marker says:

```
👉 Get the thing: (LINK: NEEDED — no store URL on file)
```

**Leave it exactly as it is** and add a comment saying the link is missing.

That marker is a deliberate stop sign. It means nobody has supplied that URL yet and the
email is not allowed to send. If you delete it or fill in a guess, the email looks
finished, goes out, and the button goes nowhere.

Never invent a URL. Not even an obvious-looking one.

---

## Step 4 — Hand it back

When the whole batch is done, message Zion with:

- "Formatted and links checked" — or which emails you couldn't finish and why
- Anything you commented on that you think is actually a problem, not just a question

That's it. You're done for the week.

---

## Quick reference

**Always:**
- Comment instead of edit when meaning is involved
- Click every link
- Leave `(LINK: NEEDED)` markers exactly where they are

**Never:**
- Rewrite a sentence
- Touch the appendix or the framework section
- Invent a URL
- Add or remove emoji
- Format the Subject or Preview text lines

---

## One thing to know about the handoff

Copying formatted text out of Google Docs and pasting it into an email builder often
drags along invisible junk — stray fonts, spans, inline styles — that can render badly in
Outlook and some phone clients.

So this is worth testing once, early: have Daniel or Sammy paste one formatted email into
GHL and send a test to themselves before anyone builds a habit around it. If it comes
through dirty, the fix is easy — paste as plain text into GHL, then re-apply the bold,
italics and links there. Same spec, different place.

Better to find that out on email one than on email forty.
