From the editor, after the week of Oct 26 pass. Chosen rates the current batch **8/10**. These notes are the gap to 10. Read them before Tuesday's write.

The point of the subject line is one thing: the reader opens it, and the email pays off what it promised in the first line. Judge every subject against that.

---

## 1. Split-test categories

Every subject should fall into one of six types, so we can test one type against another and learn which one moves clicks. Each category has a live example from this week's batch so you can see the shape.

| Category | What it does | Shape | E4L example (this week's facts only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **How-To Direct Benefit** | Promises one result, plainly | How to + a result the reader wants | "How to answer every lead before lunch" |
| **Shock and Awe** | A real number that makes them stop | A number, then the context | "Roblox creators earned $1.7 billion in 12 months" |
| **Personal Story** | Chosen's own life, one moment | I + a turn | "Nobody was clapping when I moved to LA" |
| **The Question** | Asks something they can't answer in their head | A question about their own habit | "How many times did you follow up with the last five?" |
| **The Attack** | Goes after a bad habit or belief, never a named person or company | Naming the thing they do wrong | "'Just checking in' is not a follow-up" |
| **Combine** | Curiosity plus a clear benefit, in one line | THIS + benefit, or a tease + payoff | "The follow-up habit that gets the reply. Most owners skip it." |

**The Attack** has no example in the source list you sent me. The Attack slot is open, and Chosen needs to say what he wants there. Until then, use the example above and the rule below.

### The rules inside each type

- **How-To:** the benefit has to be something the email can actually deliver. Do not promise a number we cannot back.
- **Shock and Awe:** only a real figure from a source on file. Chosen's "$55,000 from 200 people" is a template, not a fact we have. Do not write it in unless Chosen gives us that real number, in writing. Ready-to-use facts this week: $1.7 billion (Roblox, Sept 11), 534,947 (Census, Oct 14, a count of applications, not businesses), $0.10 vs $1.00 (Anthropic list price).
- **Personal Story:** one real moment from the story bank. No income claim unless Chosen gives us one. The bank has the sales floor, pro gaming, brand ambassador gigs, and the move to LA.
- **The Question:** must be something the reader can answer with a yes or no about their own business. It is not a quiz with a hidden sales pitch.
- **The Attack:** attack the habit, never a person, a brand or an industry. Ian's book warns that clickbait the body doesn't pay off destroys trust (the book's line is "faster than a politician caught in a sex scandal"). The subject must be fully paid off on the first screen of the email.
- **Combine:** curiosity has to be followed by a specific benefit. Curiosity with no payoff ("You won't believe this") is the failure mode, and it is what the book's own worst example looks like.

## 2. What separates an 8 from a 10

1. **One payoff, one open loop.** Pick one. "Roblox creators earned $1.7 billion" is a payoff. "What happened to Roblox" is a loop with no payoff. A 10 has both, and the second line is the payoff: "the follow-up habit that gets the reply" has the loop, and the body delivers "the reply".
2. **Use THIS and a specific benefit.** In the book's own test, "THIS habit will boost your score" beat "How to overcome procrastination without willpower" by 445% on clickthrough. The gap was specific ("THIS habit", "your score") against general ("How to", "without willpower"). Reach for the specific word first.
3. **Sound like someone who just saw it.** The 30 Days of Geneius list works because each line sounds like a text from a friend: "I'll skip the BS", "Can you take a look at this", "Did we just become best friends?". Our subjects should read like that. Test the sentence out loud. If it sounds like a news headline, rewrite it.
4. **Keep it short.** The Geneius list runs 3-9 words. Ours should too, unless the length is the joke.
5. **Preview text adds a beat.** It never restates the subject. The preview is the second line the reader gets, so give it the thing the subject leaves out.

## 3. What to test, and how to read it

- **Judge on clickthrough, not opens.** Ian judges his subject tests on clickthrough rate (the share who click the link), not opens. Treat opens as a weaker signal. Pick the winning category by clicks.
- **Test at least 3-4 variations at once,** one category each, same send time, same body. Our list is far smaller than Ian's (his test runs about 20,000 per variation). With a small list, test the biggest differences you can, not two near-identical lines. Don't call a winner on a small difference.
- **Personalized subjects (first name in the subject)** appear in Ian's list. Do not use them until Abdullah or Chosen confirms GHL merges the first name into the subject line, not just the body. The greeting in the body already uses it.
- **Question marks in the subject:** Ian's note is that a "??" subject line can work a few times a year, but the preview must carry the content. Do not use it by default.
