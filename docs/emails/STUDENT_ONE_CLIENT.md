## How the three things he asked for stay honest

- **"The money they could make."** This is the student list. Telling them what *they* will
  earn is an income claim, and we don't make those (`voice.md`: no income claim unless
  Chosen gives us one). So the email shows two **real** numbers instead: what one client
  made from email ($14,960, the screenshot), and what one real client paid *us*: Emergency
  Roofing, $1,000 to start + $1,000/mo for three months, off a cold email (story bank).
  It says plainly that this isn't a promise. The reader does the math. That's more
  persuasive anyway, and it's true.
- **"From anywhere."** True and specific: the person who loads our emails into GHL works
  about twelve hours ahead of Chosen, on the other side of the world. Unnamed, on purpose.
- **"That's just ONE client."** Said twice, in caps, which is the gold-standard CAPS beat.
  The email never multiplies it out ("imagine ten!"), because that turns into an income
  claim through the back door.

The ❌/✅ pair is written as Chosen talking, **not** as a quote from the book. We don't have
the book's exact lines on file, so attributing one would be making it up.
