**Preview text:** That's the whole plan. Here's what it costs.

*Notes:* The GAIN shape, compressed to ~250 words — longer than the ~200 ceiling because a
$500 ask needs more room than a $9.95 one, which `templates.md` allows and nothing else
here does. The triple promise, the named disbelief, the stuck loop, the "until now" turn
and the early link are all the template's; the five jobs, the 90-day plan and the pizza
shop are all from `voice.md` and the book, translated and never quoted. The bullets stop
at four because four is what can be sourced — see the gaps below. No "much, much more".

**⚠️ THREE THINGS BLOCK THIS SENDING:**

1. **No checkout URL exists.** `(LINK: NEEDED)` deliberately fails the build, so this
   cannot ship by accident. Abdullah has to create the $500 lifetime bundle first.
2. **No deadline.** The copy carries none on purpose. A promo open normally states when it
   ends, and this one can't until Chosen confirms the window in `PROMOS.md`. Add one
   sentence above the CTA once he does.
3. **The $2,000 is not claimed anywhere in this email**, on purpose. It is the strongest
   line available — "$500 for what normally costs $2,000" — and it stays out until Chosen
   confirms the course has genuinely sold at $2,000.

**⚠️ AND THE REAL GAP: nobody has told me what is in the course.**

The four bullets are inferred from the book's framework and the story bank, because that is
all that is on record. They may describe a course that doesn't exist. Before this ships,
Chosen needs to say what the buyer actually gets:

- Format — video lessons, a live cohort, a workbook, calls?
- How much of it, and over how long?
- What the five-jobs framework looks like inside the course, if that is even the spine
- Anything the bullets miss that is the best reason to buy

A $500 offer sold on inferred bullets is the fastest way to a refund and a reply that says
"this isn't what the email said."
