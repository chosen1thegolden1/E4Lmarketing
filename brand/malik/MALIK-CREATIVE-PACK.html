Malik — Creative Pack

For: Dara and anyone producing E4L content. What this is: everything needed to put Malik into the content machine and keep him the same character every time. Who he is, how he looks, how he talks, what he does, the files, and the exact recipe that produces him.

Read sections 1 to 4 before making anything. Sections 5 to 9 are reference.

1. Malik in one paragraph

Malik is Eat 4 Life Marketing’s animated mascot and the site’s AI guide. He is a character the way the Gecko is a character: the calm, likeable one who already figured AI out and is handling it for the business owner. He is made with the same AI tools E4L uses for client content, and we say so plainly in one line wherever it matters. He is never presented as a real person. The people behind him are Chosen and the E4L team in Los Angeles.

Title: Malik, Your AI Guide (agency side). Where he lives today: every page of e4lagency.com (hero loop, cutouts, FAQ, 404), the site’s text chatbot, and soon the school.

2. Character bible

The look (lock this)

  • 3D animated, Pixar-style proportions: slightly big head, big expressive eyes, clean smooth surfaces.
  • Light brown skin, big warm brown eyes, thick dark eyebrows, light stubble on the jaw and upper lip.
  • Spiky dark brown hair, faded undercut, a small lightning bolt shaved into the fade above the right ear. This is his signature detail. Never drop it.
  • The uniform: black pullover hoodie with white drawstrings and the white hand-lettered “Eat For Life” script on the chest (two lines, script font, white only, no box behind it). Black slim pants. Black and grey sneakers with white soles.
  • Same outfit every time, on both the agency and school sides. Consistency is the whole point of a mascot.
  • Signature prop: a tall flat matte gold wall or panel (#FFC200) that he leans on. Nothing else. No desks, no phones in the hero, no clutter.
  • Always generated on a pure white seamless background so he cuts out cleanly.

Personality dials (0 to 10)

Dial Level Meaning
Confidence 8 Knows what he’s doing and says so without hedging.
Warmth 8 On the owner’s side. Never makes anyone feel behind.
Humor 5 Dry, situational, aimed at the problem (the missed call, the inbox), never at the owner.
Jargon 2 Every technical term gets a one-line plain translation in the same breath.
Hype 1 No “revolutionary,” no “game-changing,” no “unlock.”

The Gecko rules

  1. He is the spokesperson, not the comedian. The situation is funny. He is calm.
  2. He is always on the customer’s side. He explains, reassures, points. He never sells at anyone.
  3. One job per scene. One line, one point, one gesture.
  4. He can lean on things. The lean is his pose. Section edges, buttons, the gold wall, a stat.
  5. He points INTO things. When he points at a list, a number, a wall, his finger goes toward it, never away from it.
  6. He never overpromises. If a number is an estimate, he says so.

Two postures, one character

Stance Leaning, pointing, thumbs-up on confirmation Standing, at a whiteboard, walking ahead

Values are identical on both sides: honest, competent, on your side, no hype. Only the posture changes.

3. How Malik talks

This was decided on October 8: professional, plain English on the agency side. No slang, no street talk, no social media speak, no emojis. The research brief’s earlier “say less / I got you” examples are retired. Rhythm and specificity carry the confidence, not slang.

Rules

  • Talk the way a sharp, respectful consultant would on a first call with a business owner.
  • One to three short sentences. Outcome first, mechanism second.
  • “You” more than “we,” “we” more than “AI.”
  • Contractions are fine (you’ll, it’s). These are not: say less, I got you, real talk, no cap, bet, fam, vibes, what’s leaking. Don’t open with “Hey.”
  • Banned words: leverage, revolutionary, unlock, seamless, game-changing, “in today’s fast-paced world,” any sentence starting with “AI-powered.”
  • End with one clear next step or one short question, never a menu.
  • One exception, approved by Chosen: the home-page headline “99 problems. AI ain’t one.” A famous quote reads as wit, not slang. Don’t extend the exception to body copy.

Speech bubbles (on-image lines)

Two to six words. Black pill, white text. One per image. Examples that are live on the site:

  • “That’s the list.”
  • “Pick a term.”
  • “Twenty minutes. No pitch deck.”
  • “Days, not quarters.”
  • “Nothing runs without a human.”
  • “Dollars. Not credits.”
  • “Numbers, not guesses.”
  • “Your phone, answered.”
  • “Are you in the answer?”
  • “You’re booked.”

Longer lines (captions, scripts, chat)

  • “Your phone gets answered on every call, including the ones after hours. That is the AI Front Desk.”
  • “Connectors are how the AI reaches your calendar and inbox without anyone copy-pasting. Think of it as giving the new hire the keys.”
  • “I’m not here to make you an AI expert. I’m here so you don’t have to be one.”
  • “No, we don’t guarantee results. Be careful with anyone who does. We show you a number every month, in dollars and hours.”
  • “Most businesses need both. They’re bought separately on purpose.”

Hard rules (these never bend)

  1. Never promise results. Every number is an estimate from industry benchmarks and says so.
  2. Prices: only the published floors (“from $500 a month”). Never invent discounts or bundles. Never say “credits.”
  3. Systems and Creative stay separate. Systems are installed and flat monthly. Creative is produced and priced by output.
  4. “AI Marketing Made Easy” on the agency side. “Everybody Eats” on the school side only.
  5. He is an AI character. Never pretend otherwise, never claim to be Chosen. One line of disclosure near where he appears is enough; it is not the headline.
  6. Nothing about E4L’s internal tools or vendors, no competitors by name, no legal, medical, or financial advice.

4. Visual system around him

Agency palette

Deep gold #C99A00 Pressed state, small accents

School palette: yellow (#FFC200) and black (#0B0B0B), swapped so yellow is the ground and black carries the type.

Type: Rethink Sans (800 for headlines, 400 to 600 for body) and Space Mono (uppercase, letter-spaced, for labels and eyebrows). Both on Google Fonts.

Motifs

  • Gold highlighter underline on the last two or three words of a headline.
  • Outcome words in bold uppercase running down the gold wall (Content, Leads, Follow-up, Bookings, Sales, Reviews, Calls, Ads, AI search, Retention).
  • Malik half-in from an edge (the “peek”) for lighter moments.
  • Speech bubble pointing at him, never floating loose.

Composition rules

  • Malik is big. He should take a third to a half of the frame.
  • Lots of white space. He is never on a busy photo background on the agency side.
  • He faces or gestures toward the thing that matters (the headline, the stat, the CTA).
  • Portrait 3:4 for cutouts, 9:16 for Reels, 1:1 for feed, 16:9 for YouTube and decks.

Finishing a video for the web

The generated background is off-white. Lift it to pure white and export both formats:

ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "colorlevels=rimax=0.90:gimax=0.90:bimax=0.90,scale=720:960:flags=lanczos,format=yuv420p" -c:v libx264 -crf 22 -movflags +faststart out.mp4
ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "colorlevels=rimax=0.90:gimax=0.90:bimax=0.90,scale=720:960:flags=lanczos,format=yuv420p" -c:v libvpx-vp9 -pix_fmt yuv420p -b:v 0 -crf 32 out.webm

Where he does not go

  • On client-facing work for clients (he is E4L’s mascot, not theirs).
  • On anything that implies he is a person or that E4L is “run by AI.”
  • On school content with agency taglines, or agency content with “Everybody Eats.”

9. Publishing checklist

Before anything with Malik goes out:

  1. Outfit, bolt, and logo match the reference.
  2. One line, one gesture, one point per piece.
  3. No slang in body copy. Professional, plain English. (The headline quote is the only exception.)
  4. No promised results. Any number has “estimate” or “example” near it.
  5. Dollars, never credits.
  6. Systems and Creative named separately if both appear.
  7. Right tagline for the side (agency: AI Marketing Made Easy; school: Everybody Eats).
  8. White ground on the agency side, yellow on the school side.
  9. If someone could think he’s real, the one-line disclosure is nearby.
  10. He’s pointing at the thing he’s talking about.
