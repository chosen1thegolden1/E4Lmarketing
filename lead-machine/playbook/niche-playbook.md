# NICHE PLAYBOOK (fit, entry offer, signals, queries)

## NICHE DECISION RULE (for any niche not listed)
Score any niche on four observable tests. (1) PHONE = MONEY: does a stranger's first contact usually arrive as a phone call or an estimate/quote request that the owner or crew often cannot answer live (urgent, after-hours, or seasonal surge)? (2) TICKET: is one booked job worth at least ~$300, or does one new customer start a recurring account worth $1,500+ lifetime, so one saved call per month pays for E4L? (3) AI-ANSWER DEMAND: do customers search 'X near me' / 'emergency X in {city}' / 'best X in {city}' in a way an AI engine would answer with a short list (GEO value)? (4) OWNER-RUN: is the typical operator an owner with 2-40 staff, 1-2 locations, a website, and not a franchise/consolidator brand? Pass all four = core if E4L already has templates/clients there, otherwise extend. Fail (1) or (2) = skip, which covers referral-only trades, walk-in retail, pure online-checkout ecommerce, B2B bid/RFP-driven contractors, and price-shopped sub-$150 tickets. A niche in a documented market contraction or with heavy fake-listing pollution is a skip even if it passes on paper. For ecommerce specifically: extend only if the store sells high-ticket or consultative products ($500+ orders, custom, B2B wholesale) and lists a phone number for pre-sale questions; otherwise skip. Heavy franchise share (pest, cleaning, restoration) means extend with a strict brand blocklist, never core.

Score any niche on three yes/no gates, in order. (1) PHONE = MONEY: does a new customer typically call (or submit a form that must be called back within minutes) to buy, and is the business often unable to answer because the owner/staff are hands-on with customers, after hours, or at lunch? If no, skip. (2) ONE SAVE PAYS THE FEE: is one recovered customer per month worth more than roughly $500-$1,000 (E4L's AI Front Desk price floor) in first-year or lifetime value? If no, skip; if the ticket is under $150 and bookings live on an app or Instagram, skip without further research. (3) OWNER CAN SAY YES: is the typical operator an owner-operated business with 1-3 locations, a real website, and no corporate parent, franchise system or PE platform controlling vendors? If no, skip. All three yes plus E4L already has a client, template or demo in it = core. All three yes but unproven for E4L = extend, and pick the entry offer by how they buy: voice AI demo when the office phone rings for money, speed-to-lead when leads arrive as forms/portal leads, GEO audit when the owner is reputation-driven and has strong gatekeepers. Ecommerce and other non-phone businesses pass only if they run a sales line, booking line or high-ticket consult (B2B wholesale, custom/made-to-order, local delivery or showroom brands); pure cart-and-ship stores with no phone intent are a skip.

Ask three yes/no questions about any niche. (1) Does money arrive from a stranger by phone call or inquiry form, with a ticket of $300+ or a recurring lifetime value of $1,000+? (2) Is the person who should answer usually busy with their hands on the work (bay, roof, kennel, classroom, event, crawlspace), or is the desk unstaffed evenings and weekends? (3) Would a buyer plausibly ask ChatGPT or Perplexity for "best [niche] near {city}" or "[niche] cost in {city}"? Three yes = chase it (core if E4L has a template or client, otherwise extend at fit 4). Two yes = extend at fit 3: test a batch of 10 leads before adding a template. One or zero = skip. Hard skips regardless of score: revenue is walk-in or drive-in, tickets under $100, franchise- or REIT-dominated (25+ locations), buyer is a committee or B2B with a sales cycle over 60 days, or the business has no website or no published phone number. For ecommerce specifically, apply the same test to the individual store: publishes a phone number, AOV $150+, not marketplace-only = extend; otherwise skip.
