# ATTITUDE / RECEPTIVITY MODEL (assign from public signals; 'Unclear' if evidence is thin)

## Growth-Minded Operator — outreach priority: high
Definition: An owner actively investing to grow (hiring, expanding service area or locations, running ads, chasing reviews) who treats the phone and inbound leads as revenue and buys tools that convert more of what they already pay for. The highest-yield buyer for speed-to-lead and voice AI, and a natural AI Search Visibility (GEO) add-on.
Signals: Hiring posts for production roles (techs, installers, stylists, associates, hygienists) on Indeed/LinkedIn/ZipRecruiter/Craigslist/Facebook in the last 90 days, or a 'Now Hiring' banner on the site or truck photos | Paid ads currently running: entries in Google Ads Transparency Center, active Meta Ad Library ads, Yelp 'Ad' placements, or a Google Local Services 'Google Guaranteed/Screened' badge | Review velocity of 10+ new Google reviews in the last 90 days, or review-request tooling visible (Podium/Birdeye/NiceJob widget, a 'leave us a review' page or QR) | Owner responds to most reviews (positive and negative) within days, in first person, with forward-looking language ('we're growing', 'new location', 'now serving [city]') | Website updated recently: new service or city pages, blog post within 6 months, financing offer, call-tracking numbers, tracking pixels, current copyright year | Consistent weekly social posting (before/after work, team, promos); owner's LinkedIn says Owner/Founder with growth language, podcast or local award mentions | Fleet or crew growth visible in photos, 'second location coming', new DBA or entity filings, service-area expansion announcements
Approach: Direct, numbers-first, short. Acknowledge they are already paying for calls (ads, LSA, reviews) and ask how many land in voicemail after 5pm or during a job. Offer the Revenue Leak Scorecard or a live voice demo built from their real site, then a 20-minute Game Plan Call. Respect their time: one specific observation, one ask.
Best hook: A live AI receptionist demo number built from their own website plus: 'every missed call from the ads you're already paying for gets answered and booked, or texted back in under a minute.'
Avoid: Vague 'AI transformation' language, educating them on why marketing matters, any promise of results or revenue (hard guardrail), anything that sounds like replacing their office staff, long multi-paragraph emails.

## Overwhelmed Owner-Operator — outreach priority: high
Definition: The owner is also the dispatcher, estimator and receptionist. The work is good but communication is bad because the phone rings while they are on a roof, in a treatment room or under a car. They need relief more than growth. This is the core voice AI and missed-call buyer, and the pain is visible in public reviews.
Signals: Two or more reviews in the last 24 months (Google/Yelp/Angi/Thumbtack) with reachability complaints: 'never called back', 'couldn't get through', 'voicemail was full', 'took a week to hear back', 'had to call three times', 'great work once they finally came out' | Owner responses absent or sporadic and apologetic ('sorry, we've been slammed', 'it's just me and my son', 'small shop, we do our best') | Website CTA is 'Call [first name]' or a single mobile number; no booking widget, no chat, no text-us; site copy in first person singular ('I've been doing this 20 years, call me directly') | Google Business Profile: messaging off, Q&A unanswered, hours limited or 'may differ', photos all of the owner on jobs | Hiring posts for office manager, CSR, dispatcher, receptionist or front desk, especially re-posted more than once, or a Facebook post 'looking for someone to answer phones' | Facebook posts like 'booked through next month', 'sorry for the slow replies', or Yelp/Facebook response-time badge showing days or none | Website claims '24/7' or 'same-day' while reviews describe voicemail or no answer after hours
Approach: Lead with relief, not growth, and keep it to a few lines. Cite one observation without judgment ('two reviews this spring mention calls not getting returned, which is what happens when the owner is the one on the job'). Give them the demo phone number so they can hear their own business answered. State plainly that go-live is conditional call forwarding of missed calls and nothing else for them to set up. Cold call by Sam is often better than email for this type.
Best hook: 'Call this number: it's an AI receptionist we already built for [Business] from your website. It picks up when you're on a job, qualifies the caller and books the estimate.' Pair with missed-call text-back.
Avoid: Long emails, multi-step asks, jargon (GEO, schema, automation stack, CRM), making them feel judged for the reviews, anything that adds admin work for them, asking them to fill out a form or quiz first.

## Tech-Forward Early Adopter — outreach priority: high
Definition: Already runs modern tools (online booking, text, CRM, reputation software, sometimes AI chat). Low friction to a yes, but the basics are covered, so they buy on edge and novelty. Best fit for AI Search Visibility (GEO) and AI-readable site work; voice AI only if their stack lacks 24/7 phone coverage.
Signals: Booking or scheduling software embedded on the site (Housecall Pro, Jobber, ServiceTitan scheduler, Mindbody, Vagaro, Boulevard, Zenoti, NexHealth, Calendly, OpenTable, Resy) or an ecommerce stack with reviews app, SMS opt-in and chatbot (Shopify + Klaviyo/Gorgias/Judge.me) | Live chat or AI chat widget, 'Text us' button, click-to-text number, customer portal or app, financing portal | Reputation tooling visible (Podium, Birdeye, NiceJob widgets) and fast, templated review responses; Google Business Profile messaging on and appointment link set; Apple Business Connect filled out | Modern, fast, mobile-first site with current copyright year, SSL, existing schema or FAQ markup | Reels/TikTok/YouTube Shorts, video content, replies to comments; owner's LinkedIn posts about software they switched to, industry conferences (Pantheon, Service World, dental or aesthetics tech events), or positive mentions of 'AI' or 'automation' | Business featured in a software vendor's case study or testimonial
Approach: Peer-to-peer and specific: show you looked at their stack and name it. Do not pitch what they already have. Pitch the gap: whether ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity name them for the money questions in their city. Lead with the free audit result and the competitor who is named instead. If they have booking but no after-hours phone coverage, position voice AI as the missing layer that books straight into their existing tool.
Best hook: The free AI Search Visibility audit with the real count: 'Named in X of 28 AI answers for [service] in [city]; here is who is named instead,' with screenshots.
Avoid: Explaining what a chatbot is, a generic missed-call pitch if they already have text-back, any ranking or citation promise, hype words, underestimating their knowledge, pitching AI-readable site rebuilds when their site already carries schema.

## Marketing-Burned Skeptic — outreach priority: medium
Definition: Has bought marketing before, often repeatedly (agencies, SEO retainers, lead marketplaces), and been burned. Has budget and need but zero tolerance for promises. Buys on proof they can verify themselves, control, and no lock-in.
Signals: Agency footer credit on the site differs from earlier Wayback Machine snapshots; multiple redesigns in 3 to 5 years; orphaned '/ppc' or landing pages; a blog of keyword-stuffed posts that stops abruptly | Owner posts or comments on Facebook, LinkedIn, Reddit, Nextdoor or trade forums complaining about HomeAdvisor/Angi/Thumbtack/Yelp Ads, 'marketing companies', 'SEO guys' or 'lead-gen scams' | Public Google reviews the owner has left for marketing or software vendors (1-star to an agency) | Google Ads Transparency Center or Meta Ad Library shows campaigns that ran for a while and then stopped; Yelp advertiser history ended | Website copy: 'no gimmicks', 'we don't pay for leads', 'unlike those other guys', 'real reviews from real customers' | Owner review responses that call out fake or competitor reviews with specifics, or praise from reviewers for 'honest, no upsell' | Job post for an in-house marketing coordinator following agency churn
Approach: Proof before pitch, zero promises, plain language. Lead with something verifiable in 60 seconds: the demo phone number or the GEO audit screenshots. Say explicitly what you are not doing (no long contract, no 'guaranteed leads', nothing to pay before they hear it). Invite them to poke holes. One short message; let the demo do the convincing.
Best hook: 'Nothing to buy until you've heard it: here is a live demo number already built for your business,' or 'Here are the 28 AI answers we checked, screenshots attached; you appear in 1 of them.'
Avoid: 'Guaranteed', '#1', rankings, ROI timelines or any results claim (hard guardrail), agency vocabulary (funnel, omnichannel, growth hacking), discounts or urgency pressure, bad-mouthing other agencies, drip sequences longer than two touches.

## Family-Legacy Pride Brand — outreach priority: medium
Definition: Multi-generation or long-tenured, name-on-the-truck business whose identity is reputation and relationships. Adopts tools that protect the name and serve customers 'the way we always have', not tools that change who they are. Decisions often involve family members, and a next-generation family member is frequently the real champion.
Signals: 'Family owned and operated since 19XX', 'second/third generation', founder story and family photos on the About page, surname in the business name, 'Est.' on logo or trucks | Long-tenured staff bios ('with us 22 years'); reviews that name the owner and 'his dad before him' | Community footprint: youth sports sponsorships, chamber membership, 'Best of [County]' awards, BBB accredited, Nextdoor neighborhood favorite | Owner responds to reviews personally and warmly, signs with their name, thanks customers by name | Website may be dated but maintained; copy stresses honesty, integrity, 'treat you like family', 'handshake' | Facebook-centric social with anniversary and milestone posts ('40 years'), holiday greetings; LinkedIn rarely used | Succession cue: 'proud to welcome [son/daughter] to the team' or a younger family member introduced in a new role
Approach: Respect the legacy and reference it precisely (the year, the generation, a family member named on the site). Frame as protection and continuity: callers still reach 'your' business because the receptionist is trained on their story; AI search is where the next generation of customers asks for a [trade] in [town]. Personal, slightly formal tone, no slang. If a next-generation family member is visible, consider addressing them as the champion and copying the owner.
Best hook: 'When someone asks ChatGPT for a [trade] in [town], [Competitor] is named and [Family Name] isn't. After [N] years, your name should be the answer.' Follow with a demo trained on their own story.
Avoid: 'Disrupt', 'modernize your outdated business', anything implying their way is wrong, any suggestion of replacing a long-time receptionist (position as after-hours and overflow only), generic templated openers, cheap urgency tactics.

## Absentee or Manager-Run — outreach priority: medium
Definition: The owner is not in the day-to-day: runs multiple businesses, has 2 to 6 locations, or has handed operations to a GM or office manager. Decisions are slower and go through a gatekeeper, but budgets are larger and systemization is welcome. If a private-equity roll-up owns it, the decision sits at the platform level and the lead is downgraded.
Signals: Owner's name absent from site and Google Business Profile; review responses signed 'Office Manager', 'Management' or 'The [Business] Team' in corporate tone | Secretary of State records or LinkedIn show the owner with multiple active entities or a holding company, or living in a different city or state from the business | 2 to 6 locations (not franchise), one centralized phone number, a 'corporate office' address, reviews mentioning hold music or a call center | Job posts for General Manager, Operations Manager, Regional or Call Center roles | Templated agency-built website with stock photos and no owner story | Reviews reference 'management', 'the office', 'the front desk' rather than a named owner | 'Now part of [Group]' or 'acquired by' announcements (private-equity or roll-up signal)
Approach: Two-track. Identify the operations lead or office manager as the champion (name from LinkedIn or review signatures) and the owner as the signer. Pitch as staff relief and reporting: after-hours and overflow calls across every location answered and booked, with a morning summary. Send something they can forward internally and expect a longer cycle.
Best hook: 'A demo your office manager can forward: every after-hours call across your [N] locations answered and booked, with a summary in their inbox each morning.'
Avoid: Owner-personal flattery ('I love your story'), assuming the owner answers the phone, sending an owner-style pitch to info@ only, pushing for a same-week call, treating a PE-owned roll-up as an independent (mark low or skip).

## Skeptical Old-School Traditionalist — outreach priority: low
Definition: Proud of doing things the old way and distrustful of automation and 'robots'. Word-of-mouth business, often an older owner near retirement. Can convert, but only through proof, patience and a human-first frame. Low yield for cold email; phone or referral works better.
Signals: Website copy: 'we answer our own phones', 'you'll always talk to a real person', 'no automated systems', 'no robots', 'old-fashioned service'; or a 1 to 3 page site with copyright 2012 to 2018, no SSL, no mobile layout | No review responses ever, or terse formal responses only to very negative reviews | Dormant Facebook (last post over 12 months), no Instagram, Yelp page unclaimed, fax number still listed | Landline-only phone, no texting, Google Business Profile messaging off, no ads anywhere, no LSA | Reviews: 'been going to him for 30 years', 'word of mouth only', 'doesn't advertise', 'cash or check' | License board shows license issued in the 1980s or 90s; 'retiring soon' or 'not taking on much new work' mentions
Approach: Do not lead with AI. Lead with the missed-call problem in their words (the calls that go to voicemail while they are on a job) and make clear a human still answers when they can; the system only covers what they already miss. Offer the demo as 'hear it yourself'. One short, polite note, or a cold call by Sam; do not chase.
Best hook: 'You still pick up every call you can. This only catches the ones that already go to voicemail, and books them. Hear it for yourself: [demo number].'
Avoid: Subject lines containing 'AI', 'automation' or 'bot'; hype; multi-touch pressure sequences; implying their business is behind; pitching GEO or schema (too abstract); anything that sounds like replacing a person.

## Booked-Out or Capacity-Capped — outreach priority: low
Definition: At capacity: waitlists, 'not accepting new patients or clients', booked months out. Does not want more leads, so growth pitches fall flat. May still want call deflection and waitlist capture, but rarely a cold-outreach win. Re-check in six months because capacity changes.
Signals: Site, Google Business Profile or social states 'not accepting new clients', 'booked through [month]', 'waitlist only', 'referral only', 'closed to new patients'; online booking shows no availability for 4+ weeks | Reviews: 'worth the wait', 'took three months to get in', 'hard to get an appointment'; owner responses 'we're fully booked, thank you for your patience' | No ads, no production hiring (or replacement hiring only), no growth language anywhere | Social posts apologizing for slow replies or announcing closures with no coverage | Ecommerce equivalent: perpetual 'sold out', pre-order only, limited drops, 'we cap orders'
Approach: Only with the capacity-pain angle: the phone still rings with people they cannot serve, and an AI receptionist can answer, take waitlist details, route true emergencies and stop interrupting staff. One light-touch message, no follow-up sequence, and a dated reminder to re-evaluate in six months or when hiring or expansion signals appear.
Best hook: 'You're booked out, but the phone still rings. Let it get answered, add callers to the waitlist and leave your front desk alone.'
Avoid: 'Get more customers or leads', revenue-leak framing, GEO or discovery pitches (they do not want to be found right now), urgency, repeated follow-ups.

## Defensive or Combative — outreach priority: skip
Definition: Publicly argumentative owner who fights customers in review responses, blames, threatens or signals open hostility to vendors and solicitors. Cold outreach produces spam complaints and wasted cycles, and the deliverability risk to the sending domain outweighs any upside.
Signals: Two or more owner responses in the last 24 months that attack the reviewer ('you're lying', 'we have you on camera', 'our attorney will be in touch'), name-calling, all caps, sarcasm or blaming the customer | Website, Google Business Profile or storefront photos reading 'NO SOLICITING', 'we do not respond to sales emails', 'vendors will be reported as spam'; GBP Q&A answered rudely | Business social page used for rants about customers, competitors or 'scammers', political flame wars, comments turned off after fights | BBB complaints with combative business responses; reviews describing the owner yelling, hanging up or being 'rude on the phone' | Reviews or forum posts describing the owner berating or hanging up on salespeople | Court records or local news showing suits against reviewers
Approach: Do not cold-email or cold-call. Log the evidence, mark skip, and add the PlaceId and domain to a suppression list so a fresh scrape cannot re-add them. Only a warm referral would ever reopen this.
Best hook: None. Skip.
Avoid: Any cold outreach, adding to any sequence, re-adding after a new scrape, arguing the classification without new evidence.

## SIGNAL SOURCES (ranked)
- 1. Google Business Profile: last 20 reviews plus every 1 to 2 star review in 24 months, owner responses and their timestamps, Q&A, posts, attributes (messaging on/off, appointment link), hours, photo recency, Local Services 'Google Guaranteed/Screened' badge. Highest density of attitude signal per minute and present for nearly every target.
- 2. The business website: copy voice (first person vs corporate), About/Team page (owner name, tenure, family, staff bios), Contact page (direct email vs form only, cell vs landline, text-us), tech-stack scan (booking widget, chat, review widgets, call tracking, pixels), copyright year, SSL, mobile layout, schema or FAQ markup, agency footer credit, blog recency, careers page, explicit statements such as 'no robots', 'we answer our own phones', 'no soliciting'.
- 3. Facebook and Instagram business pages: posting cadence and recency, format (reels vs text), owner voice in captions and comment replies, 'typically replies within X' badge, pinned 'booked out' or 'hiring' posts, rants or flame wars.
- 4. Hiring posts (Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs, ZipRecruiter, Craigslist, Facebook Jobs, site careers page): role type is the signal. Office, CSR, dispatcher, receptionist or front desk means phone overwhelm; techs, installers or producers means growth; GM, operations or regional means absentee. Re-posting frequency amplifies the read.
- 5. Ad presence: Google Ads Transparency Center, Meta Ad Library, Yelp ad placements, Local Services listings, Nextdoor ads. Active means investing; ran-then-stopped suggests marketing-burned; none plus old site suggests old-school.
- 6. Yelp: reviews, owner responses, response-time and response-rate stats, advertiser history. Especially useful for restaurants, salons, med spas, auto and pet grooming where Google reviews are thinner.
- 7. Vertical review and directory sites: Healthgrades/Zocdoc (dental), Avvo (law), RealSelf (med spa and plastic surgery), Angi/HomeAdvisor/Thumbtack/Houzz (home services), Zillow/Realtor.com (real estate), DealerRater/Cars.com (auto), OpenTable/TripAdvisor (restaurants), Rover (pet), Trustpilot/Judge.me/Shopify reviews (ecommerce).
- 8. Owner's LinkedIn (and public personal Facebook): title, tenure, other ventures, location vs business location, posts about tools, AI, vendors, conferences or complaints about marketing companies.
- 9. BBB profile: accreditation (reputation-conscious), complaint history, and the tone of the business's complaint responses.
- 10. Wayback Machine snapshots of the site: redesign and agency churn, removed chat or 'AI' experiments, statements like 'no robots' added or removed over time.
- 11. State license boards, Secretary of State and county DBA filings: owner's legal name, founding year, entity type, multiple entities (absentee), license age (retirement window). Used for verification and growth stage, not for tone.
- 12. Local press, podcasts, YouTube, chamber and association pages, award lists: growth and community signals, and the owner's own words on record.
- 13. Reddit, Nextdoor and trade forums: owner complaints about marketing vendors, customer threads about businesses that never call back.
- 14. After-hours test call by Sam or an authorized voice agent (optional, last, only when the owner of E4L authorizes the probe): records only whether the line hits voicemail, an IVR, an answering service or a human. Never inferred from email scraping.

## SECONDARY DIMENSIONS (tag each lead)
- tech_adoption_level: none / basic / modern / advanced / unknown — Scan site plus Google Business Profile. none = no SSL or copyright 4+ years stale, no booking, chat or text, landline only. basic = SSL, contact form, claimed GBP and Facebook, no booking. modern = online booking or scheduling software, or a text-us button, or review-request tooling, or GBP messaging on. advanced = chat or AI chat widget, customer portal or app, automation evidence (instant text after a form), multiple integrated tools, schema or FAQ markup present. Log the named tools in tech_tools_detected.
- marketing_sophistication: none / diy / agency_managed / in_house_team / unknown — none = no ads, no blog, template or stock site. diy = owner-made Canva-style posts, boosted Facebook posts, free site builder. agency_managed = agency footer credit, landing pages with call-tracking numbers, steady ad campaigns, templated review responses. in_house_team = a marketing coordinator on the team page or in a job post, branded video, email newsletter signup.
- growth_stage: new / establishing / established / plateau / scaling / unknown — From founding year (site, Secretary of State, license), total reviews and last-90-day review velocity, hiring and locations. new = under 2 years or under 25 reviews. establishing = 2 to 5 years, velocity rising, first hires. established = 5+ years, steady reviews, stable team. plateau = established but flat or declining velocity, stale site and social, no hiring. scaling = hiring both production and office roles, second location or service-area expansion, fleet growth. New-stage businesses are receptive but budget-constrained; note it in budget_risk_note.
- review_response_behavior: none / sporadic / templated / personal / defensive / unknown — Sample the last 20 reviews plus all 1 to 2 star reviews in 24 months. none = zero responses. sporadic = under 30 percent responded. templated = 50 percent or more responded with near-identical copy. personal = 50 percent or more responded with specific details or names. defensive = 2+ responses attacking the reviewer (this value overrides the others and feeds the Defensive type).
- public_response_speed: same_day / within_week / slow / never / unknown — Median gap between review date and owner reply on Google; Facebook 'typically replies within' badge; Yelp response-time stat; whether GBP Q&A is answered. never = no replies anywhere. Slow or never alongside a 24/7 claim is a pain signal, not just a tone signal.
- communication_tone: warm_personal / professional / salesy_hype / terse / combative / unknown — From website copy, owner review responses and social captions. warm_personal = first person, names, gratitude. professional = third-person corporate voice. salesy_hype = superlatives, urgency, '#1', emojis and caps. terse = one-line replies with no greeting. combative = attacks or threats. Match outreach tone to this value (mirror warm with warm, professional with professional, terse with very short).
- phone_dependence: phone_first / mixed / web_first / ecommerce_no_phone / unknown — phone_first = 'Call now' is the primary CTA above the fold, phone in the header, call-tracking numbers, emergency or same-day language, no booking widget. mixed = phone and booking or form given equal weight. web_first = booking widget primary, phone secondary. ecommerce_no_phone = no phone or support@ only, chat or email support. phone_first and mixed make voice AI the lead offer; web_first and ecommerce make GEO or AI-readable site the lead offer.
- after_hours_coverage: claims_24_7 / hours_only / answering_service / unknown — Compare site claims ('24/7 emergency', 'same day') with GBP hours and with reviews mentioning after-hours experience ('got voicemail at night', 'answering service took a message'). A 24/7 claim with voicemail reviews is a strong pain signal and a direct demo angle.
- missed_call_pain: none / mild / strong / unknown — Count reviews in the last 24 months containing reachability phrases: never called back, no answer, voicemail, couldn't reach, took days, no response to my form or text. none = 0. mild = 1. strong = 2 or more, or any owner response admitting it ('we've been slammed'), or a current hiring post for CSR, dispatcher or receptionist. Quote one review verbatim with its URL in missed_call_quote.
- ai_stance: pro / neutral / anti / unknown — pro = uses AI chat or AI booking, or says 'AI' or 'automation' positively on site, social or LinkedIn. anti = 'no robots', 'you'll always reach a real person', 'we don't use automated systems', anti-AI social posts. neutral = no statement but some automation in use. unknown = no evidence. Record the exact quote and URL in ai_stance_quote; anti shifts the hook away from the word AI and toward missed-call coverage.
- decision_maker_access: owner_direct / gatekeeper / unknown — owner_direct = owner named on site or GBP, personal email or cell visible, signs review responses. gatekeeper = office manager or front desk signs responses, info@ only, 'contact our office', multi-location. Determines who the email addresses and whether a champion must be named in champion_name.
- timing_trigger: none / hiring_now / new_location_or_expansion / bad_review_spike / seasonal_peak_near / recent_rebrand_or_new_site / new_ownership / unknown — A public event in the last 90 days that makes 'now' relevant, with its source URL in timing_trigger_source. bad_review_spike = 2+ negative reviews in 30 days with reachability themes. seasonal_peak_near = HVAC heading into summer or winter, roofing storm season, tax season for accounting, wedding season for salons and med spas, and so on. Any trigger raises outreach_priority one level.
- niche_need_fit: strong / moderate / weak — The bot's judgment for any niche, including extended ones like ecommerce. strong = appointment or estimate driven, inbound phone is the primary lead channel, an urgency or emergency component, high ticket, reviews matter, and people ask AI assistants 'best', 'near me' or 'emergency' questions about it. moderate = phone matters but bookings are mostly online, or lower ticket. weak = no inbound phone, B2B procurement, marketplace-only (Amazon-only sellers), or a vertical regulated against automated calls. Ecommerce is strong only when there is a brand-owned storefront (not marketplace-only), a visible customer-service phone or chat load (order status, returns, sizing), and products people ask AI to recommend. Record the one-sentence reason in niche_need_reason; weak means do not pull further leads in that niche.

## EVIDENCE RULE
Assign an attitude_primary only when at least 3 independent signals from at least 2 different source categories (for example website plus reviews, or reviews plus a job post) point to the same type and no strong signal contradicts it; otherwise write 'unclear'. Each signal must be logged with a source URL, the date observed, and a verbatim quote or exact observation (never a paraphrase, never inferred from the niche, the business name, a photo or a stereotype). Confidence: high = 4+ signals across 3 source categories; medium = 3 signals across 2 categories; anything less is 'unclear'. When two types tie, record the stronger as attitude_primary and the other as attitude_secondary (common pairs: Family-Legacy + Overwhelmed, Growth-Minded + Tech-Forward, Marketing-Burned + Old-School). Signals older than 24 months count at half weight; review-response patterns require at least 10 reviews in the last 24 months before they count at all. Asymmetric rule for Defensive or Combative: 2 strong signals from any single source are enough to mark skip, because the cost of outreach to a hostile owner (spam complaints against the sending domain) far exceeds the cost of a missed lead. Secondary dimensions are filled independently of the attitude type: each needs at least 1 direct observation or is left 'unknown'; a blank beats a guess. Priority adjustment after typing: start from the type's default outreach_priority; raise one level if missed_call_pain is strong or any timing_trigger is present; lower one level if ai_stance is anti or decision_maker_access is gatekeeper with no champion_name found; force skip if chain or franchise (25+ locations), no website, PE or roll-up owned, or Defensive. An 'unclear' type defaults to medium when niche_need_fit is strong and phone_dependence is phone_first or mixed and missed_call_pain is at least mild, otherwise low, and always uses the generic hook (live voice demo built from the site) in a neutral professional tone. Never fabricate a signal to reach the threshold; if the bot cannot reach 3 signals in a reasonable search, 'unclear' is the correct and expected answer.
